    GPT-5 as your default. This is the smartest model OpenAI makes.

CHAPTER 2 — GPT-5 in Real Use  [2:30 to 3:45]
Show: Typing a prompt into ChatGPT, showing the response
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
    Let me show you what GPT-5 actually feels like rather than
    just describing it.

    I asked it to write an email declining a freelance project —
    politely, but keeping the door open for future work.

    [pause — show the response on screen]

    Read that. That sounds like a person wrote it. Not a template.
    Not corporate-speak. An actual human email.

    The thing GPT-5 does better than earlier versions is following
    complex instructions without losing the thread. Tell it something
    has five requirements and it actually handles all five.
    Earlier models would nail three and subtly miss the other two.

    That said — it still makes things up. It'll confidently tell you
    a statistic that doesn't exist or misquote something. This hasn't
    been fixed. If you're using ChatGPT for anything factual —
    research, data, quotes — verify it before you use it.
    Every time. That's not negotiable.


CHAPTER 3 — The Memory Feature  [3:45 to 5:00]
Show: ChatGPT memory settings page
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
    I dismissed Memory when it launched. I thought it was a gimmick.

    I was wrong.

    Here's what I have saved in mine: that I run a blog about AI tools,
    that I prefer concise answers without excessive bullet points, that
    I'm reviewing tools from a user perspective not a technical one.

    Now every conversation I start — ChatGPT already knows those things.
    I don't re-explain my context every single time.

    For writers, for freelancers, for anyone doing the same type of work
    repeatedly — this compounds. After a month of use your ChatGPT is
    tuned to you in a way that a new user's isn't.

    You can see everything it remembers. Edit it. Delete specific memories.
    You're in full control.

    This is the feature I didn't expect to be one of my favourites. It is.


CHAPTER 4 — o1 and o3-mini  [5:00 to 6:00]
Show: Switching model to o1 in ChatGPT, showing the "Thinking..." phase
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
    Inside ChatGPT Plus there are also two models called o1 and o3-mini.
    These work completely differently from GPT-5.

    Standard GPT-5 answers you immediately. o1 actually thinks before
    it responds. You'll see a Thinking phase — sometimes 20 or 30 seconds
    — where it reasons through the problem step by step.

    For a simple email? Don't bother, it's overkill.

    For debugging complex code, solving a tricky logic problem, or
    analysing something with a lot of moving parts? It's noticeably better.

    I've had o1 catch errors in code that GPT-5 missed entirely.
    It's a different tool for a different job — and it's included in your $20.


CHAPTER 5 — Where It Falls Short  [6:00 to 7:00]
Show: Neutral screen or on-camera
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
    I'm not going to just sell you on this.

    Hallucination. Still the main issue. Still unresolved. ChatGPT will
    give you wrong information with complete confidence. The web search
    feature helps — but it doesn't fix the problem.

    Image generation. DALL-E 3 is good. But for artistically impressive
    images — Midjourney produces better results. I'll link that
    comparison below.

    Context limits. On very long conversations the quality starts to slip.
    It doesn't handle multi-hour research sessions as well as short
    focused tasks.

    These aren't deal-breakers for most people. But you should know
    they exist.


CHAPTER 6 — Who Should Pay  [7:00 to 8:00]
Show: Simple text list on screen
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
    Here's the honest answer on who should pay.

    Worth it if:
    You write regularly — emails, content, documentation.
    You code and want instant debugging help.
    You use AI more than 4 or 5 times per week.

    Probably fine with free if:
    You use AI occasionally.
    You only need simple questions answered.
    You're just getting started with AI.

    If you're not sure — use the free tier for one week. Notice how often
    you hit the usage limit or wish the answer was sharper.
    That frustration is your answer.
