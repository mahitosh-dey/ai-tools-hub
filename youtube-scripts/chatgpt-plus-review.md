**Purpose:** Full review. Build trust. Drive viewers to the blog for pricing tables + links.

>
> Let's get into it."

---

### [INTRO — 0:30 to 1:00]
*[Show channel name / simple intro card]*

> "Quick context: I run a blog called AI Vault where I review AI tools with actual daily use — not just a 20-minute test. Link's in the description if you want the written version of this review with all the pricing tables.
>
> Now. ChatGPT Plus. What do you actually get?"

---

### [CHAPTER 1: What Plus Gives You — 1:00 to 2:30]
*[Screen record: ChatGPT Plus feature list on OpenAI's site]*

> "The free tier of ChatGPT still exists and it's genuinely useful. So the question is — what does that extra $20 buy?
>
> Here's the list that actually matters.
>
> GPT-5 as your default. This is the smartest model OpenAI makes. The free tier has GPT-5 too, but with strict daily limits. Hit that limit and it downgrades you to an older model mid-day. That's frustrating when you're in the middle of something.
>
> DALL-E 3 image generation — built right into the chat. You describe what you want, it generates it. No separate tool, no extra subscription.
>
> Advanced Voice Mode. You can have an actual back-and-forth spoken conversation with ChatGPT — interrupting it mid-sentence, asking follow-ups, changing direction. It's not perfect but it's genuinely different from any voice assistant I've used.
>
> Memory. ChatGPT remembers things about you across every conversation. I'll come back to this one — it's the feature that actually changed how I use the product day to day.
>
> Web search, file uploads, Canvas for editing long documents, Projects for organising work by topic.
>
> That's a lot. Free ChatGPT is a calculator. Plus is closer to an actual assistant."

---

### [CHAPTER 2: GPT-5 in Real Use — 2:30 to 3:45]
*[Screen record: Typing prompt into ChatGPT, showing response]*

> "Let me show you what GPT-5 actually feels like rather than just describing it.
>
> I asked it to write an email declining a freelance project — politely, but keeping the door open for future work.
>
> [pause — show the response on screen]
>
> Read that. That sounds like a person wrote it. Not a template. Not corporate-speak. An actual human email.
>
> The thing GPT-5 does better than earlier versions is following complex instructions without losing the thread. Tell it something has five requirements and it actually handles all five. Earlier models would nail three and subtly miss the other two.
>
> That said — it still makes things up. It'll confidently tell you a statistic that doesn't exist or misquote something. This hasn't been fixed. If you're using ChatGPT for anything factual — research, data, quotes — verify it before you use it. Every time. That's not negotiable."

---

### [CHAPTER 3: The Memory Feature — 3:45 to 5:00]
*[Screen record: ChatGPT memory settings page]*

> "I dismissed Memory when it launched. I thought it was a gimmick.
>
> I was wrong.
>
> Here's what I have saved in mine: that I run a blog about AI tools, that I prefer concise answers without excessive bullet points, that I'm reviewing tools from a user perspective not a technical one.
>
> Now every conversation I start — ChatGPT already knows those things. I don't re-explain my context every single time.
>
> For writers, for freelancers, for anyone doing the same type of work repeatedly — this compounds. After a month of use your ChatGPT is tuned to you in a way that a new user's isn't.
>
> You can see everything it remembers. Edit it. Delete specific memories. You're in full control.
>
> This is the feature I didn't expect to be one of my favourites. It is."

---

### [CHAPTER 4: o1 and o3-mini — 5:00 to 6:00]
*[Screen record: Switching model to o1, showing "Thinking..." phase]*

> "Inside ChatGPT Plus there are also two models called o1 and o3-mini. These work completely differently from GPT-5.
>
> Standard GPT-5 answers you immediately. o1 actually *thinks* before it responds. You'll see a Thinking phase — sometimes 20 or 30 seconds — where it reasons through the problem step by step.
>
> For a simple email? Don't bother, it's overkill.
>
> For debugging complex code, solving a tricky logic problem, or analysing something with a lot of moving parts? It's noticeably better.
>
> I've had o1 catch errors in code that GPT-5 missed entirely. It's a different tool for a different job — and it's included in your $20."

---

### [CHAPTER 5: Where It Falls Short — 6:00 to 7:00]
*[On-camera or voiceover on neutral screen]*

> "I'm not going to just sell you on this.
>
> Hallucination. Still the main issue. Still unresolved. ChatGPT will give you wrong information with complete confidence. The web search feature helps — but it doesn't fix the problem.
>
> Image generation. DALL-E 3 is good. But for artistically impressive images — Midjourney produces better results. I'll link that comparison below.
>
> Context limits. On very long conversations the quality starts to slip. It doesn't handle multi-hour research sessions as well as short focused tasks.
>
> These aren't deal-breakers for most people. But you should know they exist."

---

### [CHAPTER 6: Who Should Pay — 7:00 to 8:00]
*[Simple on-screen text list]*

> "Here's the honest answer on who should pay.
>
> Worth it if: you write regularly — emails, content, documentation. You code and want instant debugging help. You use AI more than 4 or 5 times per week.
>
> Probably fine with free if: you use AI occasionally. You only need simple questions answered. You're just getting started with AI.
>
> If you're not sure — use the free tier for one week. Notice how often you hit the usage limit or wish the answer was sharper. That frustration is your answer."

---

### [VERDICT + CTA — 8:00 to 8:45]
*[On-camera or clean screen]*

> "My verdict: 8.5 out of 10.
>
> ChatGPT Plus is worth $20 a month if AI is genuinely part of how you work. The gap between free and Plus is large — you feel it immediately.
>
> The half point it loses is for hallucination. When it's good it's genuinely impressive. Just don't turn your brain off while you're using it.
>
> Full written review with the pricing table, every feature compared, and my exact setup is at aivaultblog.com — link in the description, first line.
>
> If this helped, subscribe — I review AI tools like this every week. See you in the next one."
