If you build on an AI API, you have six days before the first of ten published changes lands.

I wrote this post on 16 November and counted four dated changes. That was wrong, and not because the vendors moved. It was wrong because I read the pricing pages and not the deprecation pages, and the most consequential thing happening in December is not on a pricing page at all.

Three of the ten are not price rises. They are model shutdowns with automatic rerouting, which means your code keeps working, returns no error, and gets answered by a different model.

## Three of these are not price changes

Start here, because this is the part a price table cannot show you.

On **1 December 2026**, Google shuts down two models and reroutes their traffic. From its deprecations page: requests to `gemini-2.5-pro` automatically route to `gemini-3.8-flash`, and requests to `gemini-2.5-flash` automatically route to `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`.

On **3 December 2026**, the same thing happens to video. The notice on Google's pricing page is the most explicit of the three, so it is worth quoting exactly: requests to `gemini-omni-1.1-flash` "automatically route to `gemini-omni-1.2-flash` ... and are billed at `gemini-omni-1.2-flash` rates."

Read that last clause again. The reroute is not a courtesy that preserves your pricing. It moves you onto the replacement's rate card.

Here is why this is worse than a price rise, even though it sounds smaller.

A price rise is loud. Your bill goes up, you notice, you investigate. A silent reroute is quiet in every system you have. Your error rate does not move. Your latency might not move. Your request count is identical. The thing that changes is which model is generating your output, and nothing in your stack is watching for that.

If you have ever evaluated model output for your use case, picked `gemini-2.5-pro` because it did something the alternatives did not, and written that decision down in a document somewhere, that decision expires on 1 December. Nothing breaks. The evaluation just stops describing what your product does.

Then **18 December**, three text-to-speech preview models shut down: `gemini-3.1-flash-tts-preview`, `gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts` and `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts`, with `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` or `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` named as replacements.

One caveat I want to put right next to this rather than at the bottom. Google's deprecations page describes its shutdown dates as "earliest possible dates." So 1 December is the earliest your traffic moves, not a guarantee that it does. That is a reasonable thing for a vendor to say and an awkward thing to plan against, and it sits oddly beside a pricing page that prints exact figures for exact days.

Two more sit just outside the window and are worth knowing now. OpenAI's Ultrafast tier for GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol costs three times the standard rate through 28 February 2027 and six times from 1 March 2027. And `gemini-2.5-flash-image` shuts down on 15 March 2027, rerouting to `gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image`.

Anthropic is not on this list at all, and that is a change from what I wrote last week. More on that below.

## Google tells you exactly what is coming

I want to credit the vendor doing the pricing half of this well, because it is rare enough to be worth saying, even while the shutdown half of the same company's disclosure is buried on a different page.

Google's pricing page does not footnote the increase or hide it in a changelog. It prints both numbers in the same cell. Gemini 4 Carbon's input reads "$2.00 through November 30, 2026." directly above "$4.00 starting December 1, 2026." Output says $10.00 then $20.00.

The doubling is wider than I reported last week. It is not just the standard rate. Every tier goes with it:

| Carbon tier | Through 30 Nov | From 1 Dec |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | $2.00 / $10.00 | $4.00 / $20.00 |
| Batch | $1.00 / $5.00 | $2.00 / $10.00 |
| Flex | $1.00 / $5.00 | $2.00 / $10.00 |
| Priority | $3.60 / $18.00 | $7.20 / $36.00 |
| Context caching | $0.10 | $0.20 |

Gemini 4 Argon carries the identical schedule. If you moved to Batch or Flex to save money, you have not escaped the increase, you have just halved it in absolute terms.

Then on 1 January 2027, Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.6 Flash double from $0.75 and $3.75 to $1.50 and $7.50, with their Batch, Flex and Priority tiers doubling alongside, plus context caching and the hourly storage price, which goes from $0.50 to $1.00 per million tokens per hour.

You can budget against all of that. You know the numbers, you know the days, and you have time to test whether a cheaper model does your job.

Two escape routes exist and neither was in last week's version of this post. **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** is $1.50 and $7.50 with no scheduled change, and it is available on the free tier, which Carbon and Argon are not. **Gemini 4 Neon** went generally available on 18 November at $3.00 and $15.00, also with no dated change on the page. Neon is the obvious landing spot if Carbon's quality is what you need and $4 is what you cannot pay, and it did not exist when I first wrote this.

Doubling a price is not generous. Publishing it with the figure attached is the minimum a vendor owes someone who built a product on their rate card. Google cleared that bar on price and did not clear it on shutdowns, which is a strange way to run one company's documentation.

## OpenAI gives you the deadline and keeps the number

I re-read this page today specifically to see whether it had changed. It has not.

OpenAI's developer pricing page still carries exactly this: "GPT-5.6 Sol's promotional pricing is available until November 30, 2026."

That is the whole disclosure, six days out. The page does not say what the price becomes on 1 December. It does not mark which of its published figures is the promotional one. The standard table lists `gpt-5.6-sol` at $4 input and $20 output, and whether that is the promotion or the destination is not stated anywhere on the page.

So you are told to prepare, and not told what for. Eight days of waiting has not produced the number.

The same page does it again with the specialised models: GPT-6 Astra for Law, GPT-6.2 Astra for Law and GPT-6 Rosalind have promotional pricing "available at least through December 31, 2026." At least through. No end date, no end price, two pieces of information missing from one sentence.

I am not accusing anyone of anything. Promotional pricing is a normal commercial decision and a vendor is entitled to reprice. But there is a real difference between Google printing the future number in the same cell as the current one and OpenAI printing a date with no number, and the difference costs you the ability to plan.

One correction to last week's version, on a detail I was slightly too confident about. I wrote that OpenAI's long context rate applies above 272,000 input tokens. The page actually says "Short context: 272K input tokens" and above that it is long context. It writes **272K** and never defines whether K means 1,000 or 1,024. If you are sizing prompts against that boundary deliberately, the difference is about 5,000 tokens of headroom you cannot confirm you have.

The long context premium itself is real and roughly doubles your rate. GPT-6 Astra goes from $10 and $50 to $20 and $75. GPT-6.1 Sol goes from $2 and $10 to $4 and $15. GPT-5.6 Sol goes from $4 and $20 to $8 and $30.

## The price cut that was not a cut

This is the section I got wrong last week, so I am rewriting it rather than patching it.

I wrote that Anthropic cut its API prices, that Opus went from $5 to $4 per million input tokens, and that the tokenizer change clawed the cut back. The arithmetic was right. The premise was not.

Anthropic's pricing page carries **no change notice for Opus**. Here is what it actually lists today:

| Model | Input / Output per MTok |
|---|---|
| Claude Opus 5.5 | $4 / $20 |
| Claude Opus 5 | $5 / $25 |
| Claude Opus 4.8 | $5 / $25 |
| Claude Opus 4.7 | $5 / $25 |
| Claude Opus 4.6 | $5 / $25 |
| Claude Opus 4.5 | $5 / $25 |
| Claude Opus 4.1 (retired) | $15 / $75 |
| Claude Opus 4 (retired) | $15 / $75 |

Nothing was repriced. Opus 5 is still $5 and still on the page. Opus 5.5 is a newer, separate row at $4. If you are running on Opus 5 today, your rate has not moved and will not move because of anything on this page.

So the honest version of the finding is not "a cut that costs you more." It is this: **the cheaper new model is not cheaper for the same document, if you are coming from the right place.**

Anthropic's page says: "Claude 4.7 and later models and Claude Mythos Preview use a newer tokenizer that contributes to their improved performance on a wide range of tasks. This tokenizer produces approximately 30% more tokens for the same text." And: "Claude Sonnet 4.6 and earlier models use the previous tokenizer."

Tokens are the unit you are billed in. So run the arithmetic on a migration from Opus 4.6, which is on the old tokenizer, to Opus 5.5, which is on the new one. You pay 20 percent less per token, so multiply by 0.8. The same document produces about 30 percent more tokens, so multiply by 1.3. That lands at 1.04, or roughly **4 percent more** for the same work.

Every figure involved went down. The bill went up.

### Where it does not bite

This only applies to migrations that cross the tokenizer boundary at Claude 4.7. Opus 4.7, 4.8 and 5 were all already on the new tokenizer at $5. Moving from any of those to Opus 5.5 at $4 is a clean 20 percent saving with no catch.

And one migration gets better rather than worse. Sonnet 4.6 is $3 and $15 on the old tokenizer. Sonnet 5.5 is $2 and $10 on the new one. That is 0.667 multiplied by 1.3, about 0.87, so roughly 13 percent cheaper for the same text even after the token inflation.

Fable moved the same way: Fable 5.1 and Fable 5 are both $10 and $50, and Fable 5.2 arrived at $8 and $40. Same pattern, same caveat, and again not a repricing of anything.

The lesson is not that Anthropic is being sneaky. It is that a rate card stopped being enough information to compare two models, and you now have to know which tokenizer each one uses before the numbers mean anything.

One thing I need to withdraw. Last week I wrote that Anthropic's page describes a million tokens as about 555,000 words, down from roughly 750,000 on the old tokenizer, and used it to illustrate the same change from the other direction. **I cannot find any token-to-word conversion on that page today.** No word count, no character count, nothing that converts the two. I am not going to assert that it changed in eight days when the simpler explanation is that I took the figure from somewhere else and recorded it carelessly. The tokenizer point stands on the 30 percent sentence, which is quoted above and is on the page. I have written up how far vendors' own context figures drift from each other in [what the 1M context claims actually mean](/blog/ai-context-window-claims-2026).

## The 10 cent model that costs 50 cents

Haiku deserves its own section, because the tenfold cut is real and the headline is still misleading.

Anthropic's pricing page lists Haiku 5.5 twice. Prompts up to 100,000 tokens cost $0.10 per million input and $0.50 output. Prompts over 100,000 tokens cost $0.50 input and $2.50 output.

Five times more, same model, same page.

The page is specific about how the line is drawn: "A request's prompt length counts all of its input tokens, including cache reads and cache writes. Each request is priced on its own: a request over the threshold pays the higher prices even when part of its prompt is a cache hit, and earlier requests keep the prices they were billed at."

So one oversized request is billed at the higher rate, and a workload that mostly sits under the line but occasionally goes over does not average out the way you would hope. Cache hits do not rescue you either, because they count toward the length that decides the rate.

Below 100,000 tokens, Haiku 5.5 really is about 87 percent cheaper than Haiku 4.5 for the same text, even after the tokenizer adjustment. Above it, the saving drops to roughly 35 percent. Still a cut. Not the one on the front of the page.

This is not the first time I have found a vendor's real price sitting one click below its headline. I went through seven vendors looking for exactly this in my [pricing page versus documentation audit](/blog/ai-pricing-page-vs-docs-audit).

## Where the token cliffs are

Here is the comparison I wish someone had handed me, because all three vendors solved the long-prompt problem differently and none of it is visible from a headline rate.

**OpenAI charges a premium above the line.** Above 272K input tokens you move to long context rates, which roughly double input and add 50 percent or so to output, per request.

**Google tiers one model by prompt length.** Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview is $2 and $12 for prompts up to 200k tokens and $4 and $18 above it. Its other models are flat.

**Anthropic mostly charges nothing extra.** Its page says "Claude 4.6 and later models (except Claude Haiku 5.5) and Claude Mythos Preview include the full 1M token context window at standard pricing," and spells out the consequence: "A 900k-token request is billed at the same per-token rate as a 9k-token request." The exception is the one that trips people, and it is Haiku, the cheap model people reach for when processing volume.

So the vendor with the cheapest small-prompt model is the only one of the three that penalises long prompts on that specific model. If your plan was to run large documents through the cheapest thing available, check which side of 100,000 tokens you are on before you commit.

## Where the vendors contradict themselves

Two of these are worth recording, because they are the reason I will not treat any of this as settled until the day it happens.

**Google says the 2.5 models are and are not deprecated.** Its changelog entry from 18 September says of Gemini 2.5 Pro and 2.5 Flash that "These models are not deprecated and will continue to be served until further notice through the API." Its entries from 2 November say both are deprecated and no longer selectable in AI Studio, with shutdowns on 1 December. Both statements are live on Google's own site today. The November one is presumably current, and a reader who found the September one first has no way to know that.

**Google's own dates disagree about an earlier model.** A changelog entry says the `gemini-omni-flash-preview` endpoint "will be deprecated on September 30, 2026." The deprecations table gives that model a shutdown date of 22 October 2026. Both are in the past and it does not matter now, which is exactly why it is useful: it tells you the two pages are maintained separately.

I keep a running list of this kind of thing in [the pricing page versus documentation audit](/blog/ai-pricing-page-vs-docs-audit), and the pattern is consistent across vendors rather than specific to Google.

## What genuinely did get cheaper

I have spent most of this post on increases, so let me be straight about the direction of the last two years, because it is strongly downward and the post would be dishonest without it.

Anthropic still lists Claude Opus 4 and Opus 4.1 at $15 and $75 per million tokens, both marked retired. Today's Opus 5.5 is $4 and $20. That is about 73 percent off per token, and even after allowing for 30 percent more tokens per document it comes to roughly 65 percent less for the same work.

On OpenAI's side, GPT-4o was $2.50 and $10. GPT-6 Luna today is $0.10 and $0.50.

Anthropic also cancelled a planned increase rather than quietly applying it. Its page carries a footnote saying Sonnet 5's $2 and $10 pricing, announced as introductory through 31 August 2026, is now the standard price, and that the scheduled rise to $3 and $15 on 1 September "will not occur." That is the good version of this behaviour: a promotional rate that ended by becoming permanent, with the outcome published.

It is also the direct answer to OpenAI's missing number. A promotional rate does not have to end in an increase. It can end by becoming the price. Anthropic published that outcome. OpenAI has published a date and six days.

So intelligence per dollar collapsed through 2026. What changed this autumn is the direction, and that two vendors have put dates on it while the third has put a date on nothing.

## What to do before 30 November

Short list, and none of it takes long.

**Find out what you actually call.** Not what you think you call. Check your logs. I have watched people budget against a model they stopped using in March.

**Check whether you call anything on the shutdown list.** `gemini-2.5-pro` and `gemini-2.5-flash` on 1 December, `gemini-omni-1.1-flash` on 3 December, the three TTS previews on 18 December. These calls will keep succeeding, so nothing will alert you. This is the one item on this list that no amount of bill-watching will catch for you.

**Check whether you cross either cliff.** Over 100,000 tokens on Claude Haiku costs five times as much. Over 272K tokens on an OpenAI model roughly doubles the input rate. Both are per request, so an average will not tell you.

**If you are on Gemini 4 Carbon or Argon, your bill doubles on 1 December.** Hydrogen is $1.50 and $7.50 with nothing scheduled and is on the free tier. Neon is $3.00 and $15.00 with nothing scheduled. Test one of them this week rather than next month.

**Screenshot the pricing pages today.** Once a price changes, the old figure stops being retrievable, and you will want it when you are reconciling a bill in February. This is tedious advice and I have regretted not taking it.

**Count tokens on your real prompts before migrating Claude models.** The tokenizer difference depends on what your text looks like. Measure rather than assume.

## What I could not check, and what I got wrong

Last week this section said OpenAI's pricing pages returned 403 to an automated request, and that everything about OpenAI in this post was therefore from developer documentation only. **That is no longer true and I want to correct it clearly.** Today `platform.openai.com/docs/pricing` redirects to `developers.openai.com` and answers. `openai.com/api/pricing` answers. OpenAI's help centre answers. There was no 403 anywhere on this round.

A status code describes a server on a day, not a company's policy, and I should not have carried the 403 forward as though it were a standing fact about OpenAI.

What I genuinely could not verify: about a third of Google's pricing page, past roughly 80,000 characters, which I was unable to read as source text. The figures I have quoted all come from the portion I read directly or from the deprecations page and changelog, which I read in full. Anything in that unread tail is not in this post.

One oddity on the OpenAI consumer side, since people ask. Its consumer pricing page now answers automated requests and prints plan names with no prices next to them. The figures live in the help centre instead. I went through that in the [ChatGPT Plus review](/blog/chatgpt-plus-review) and in [ChatGPT vs Google Gemini](/blog/chatgpt-vs-google-gemini), where Google turns out to do the same thing.
