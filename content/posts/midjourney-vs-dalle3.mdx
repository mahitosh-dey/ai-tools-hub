OpenAI will show you an advertiser's pictures while it makes yours. That is the part of this comparison that changed, and it is a stranger development than the one everybody wrote about.

The thing everybody wrote about was DALL-E going away. That is now settled, and I will deal with it in a paragraph rather than a countdown.

## DALL-E is not retiring, it is absent

Earlier versions of this post hedged, and the hedge was right at the time. In August 2026 OpenAI had announced the DALL-E retirement date, the page still resolved, and OpenAI was calling DALL-E its legacy image model. On the way out was the honest description.

It is not needed now. I searched the full text of OpenAI's pricing page and its models page on November 23, 2026. The string **DALL** does not appear on either one. Not as deprecated, not as legacy, not as sunsetting. There is no row for it. The API was shut down separately back on May 12, 2026.

What sits there instead is a line of models named gpt-image, eight IDs on the pricing page, priced per million tokens with no per-image figure published for any of them. I went through what those are called and why the naming is a mess in [Google has five image models and they are all called Nano Banana](/blog/ai-image-model-names-2026).

So if you came here to find out whether DALL-E still exists: it does not. If you came to find out whether you can still make images in ChatGPT: you can, and that never stopped.

## The ad format nobody mentioned

Here is the sentence from OpenAI's own help page on ads, which is the reason this post needed rewriting rather than patching:

> We are also testing a multi-image visual ad format during image generation in ChatGPT.

And the practical version of it, from the same page:

> you may see an ad with several images from the same advertiser while ChatGPT generates your image

The ad goes away when your image finishes. OpenAI says ads are labelled and shown separately from the image being created.

This runs on the Free and Go plans. Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu are ad-free, which OpenAI states plainly in both its announcement and its help page.

I want to be careful about how new this is. Ad testing in ChatGPT started in the United States on February 9, 2026, and the announcement's August 11 update adds the United Kingdom, Mexico, Brazil, Japan and South Korea. Personalised ads are not initially available in the European Economic Area or Switzerland. So depending on where you are, you may see none of this. The two OpenAI pages also disagree with each other about logged-out users: the announcement describes the test as being for logged-in adults, while the more recently updated help page refers to ads shown in logged-out experiences. I am not going to resolve that for them. I am telling you it is unresolved on their own pages.

## The opt-out is where it gets awkward

There is an Ads-Free option. Read what it is before you relax.

> Ads-Free is a Free-plan option. If you're on a paid plan, you'd need to switch back to Free to use it, or upgrade to a plan that's already ad-free (like Plus or Pro).

And what Ads-Free costs you on the Free plan, again from OpenAI's page: lower usage limits and reduced feature access, "for example, fewer messages and no access to some tools like image generation or deep research."

Follow that through for someone who wants to make pictures without paying.

On the Free plan you can have image generation with ads, or no ads without image generation. Those are the two doors. On Go at $8 a month you are paying, and you still get ads, and Ads-Free is not available to you because it is a Free-plan option. The first ad-free plan that generates images is Plus at $20.

Midjourney's cheapest plan is $10 and shows you nothing.

I am not claiming OpenAI set out to make the $8 plan the worst spot on the grid. I am saying that is where it currently sits for this specific job, and that no comparison written before August 2026 could have told you so.

## What this comparison can and cannot tell you

The original version of this post described two weeks of side by side testing across six prompt categories, with hit rates for hands, text accuracy and prompt adherence.

That testing was real and it is thoroughly out of date on both sides. It ran against DALL-E 3 and against Midjourney around v6.1. Midjourney has since shipped V8 in March 2026, V8.1 in April, and V8.2 became the default on July 24, 2026. The DALL-E side of the comparison no longer exists as a product.

I am not going to take findings from an older generation of both models and present them as current. That is the single most common thing wrong with AI tool comparisons, and I would rather publish a shorter honest post than a detailed dishonest one. I went and counted how widespread the problem is in [the stale articles audit](/blog/ai-model-articles-stale-audit): six of seven readable articles ranking for best AI model for coding named a superseded model.

What survives is the structural part of the comparison, meaning the things that come from how the products are built rather than from a model version.

## They want to be prompted differently

This has held across every version of both tools and I expect it to keep holding.

Midjourney responds to atmosphere. Words about mood, light quality, film stock, era and art style push it somewhere useful. Something like "moody Nordic forest at dusk, mist, cinematic composition" gives you close to what those words suggest, because the model is tuned to interpret aesthetic direction. It also takes parameters, so aspect ratio and how much creative licence to allow are dials rather than sentences.

ChatGPT responds to description. Explain the picture the way you would to someone who cannot see it: who is in it, where they are, what the light is doing, what words should appear. It also expands your prompt internally, which is why you sometimes get details you did not ask for.

Paste a Midjourney prompt into ChatGPT and you get something competent and a bit generic. Paste a ChatGPT prompt into Midjourney and you get something literal and visually flat. People conclude one tool is worse when what actually happened is that they used the other tool's language.

The iteration difference follows from this. In a chat you refine by conversation: same scene, move the subject, change the season. With Midjourney you refine by re-prompting and by parameters, which is more precise once learned and less obvious before.

## What each side structurally cannot do

Midjourney does not give you conversational editing of an existing image, and it is not the tool for inpainting or fine local edits the way a Stable Diffusion setup is. There is no general assistant attached to it.

I need to correct something earlier versions of this post said here. I described Midjourney as a subscription that does exactly one thing. That is no longer true. It has an image-to-video model, taking a still and animating it, with reported clips of roughly five seconds extendable to about twenty, at 480p or 720p. Which plans include how much of it is reported inconsistently across the trackers I can read, and I could not check it against Midjourney's own documentation, so treat the detail as unconfirmed and the existence of the feature as certain.

ChatGPT's image generation is more filtered, so moody or edgy prompts get softened or declined more often. It does not offer Midjourney's style reference parameters for steering output from an uploaded image, and it has no equivalent of stealth mode, which matters if you do not want your prompts visible in a public gallery.

If neither fits, the alternatives split by what you need. Adobe Firefly for commercial safety, because it trained on licensed stock. Ideogram when the image is mostly typography. Stable Diffusion locally when you want total control and will run it yourself. Several free options are in [best free AI image generators](/blog/best-free-ai-image-generators), with the caveat that free tiers in this category keep moving.

## What actually changed about the money

A year ago both sides of this comparison cost money and the only question was which $20 to spend. That is not the shape of it now.

ChatGPT's pricing page still lists image generation on the Free plan, described as limited image creation. Go at $8 gets more, Plus at $20 gets "more complex and accurate image creation", Pro gets unlimited and faster. Image generation with Thinking, the mode where the model plans before it draws, is a Plus and Pro feature: the pricing page prints no for Free and no for Go.

The Pro tier is three tiers, which I had wrong in an earlier version of this post. OpenAI's help page names them and prices them in USD: Pro 100 at $100 a month, Pro 200 at $200, Pro 500 at $500. Billed monthly, no annual option. Plus is $20 and Go is $8. I went through what each one is actually worth in the [ChatGPT Plus review](/blog/chatgpt-plus-review).

Midjourney is reported at four tiers of $10, $30, $60 and $120 a month, with about 20 percent off annually, and fast GPU time of roughly 3.3, 15, 30 and 60 hours. Standard at $30 is where most working users land, because relaxed generation stops being metered and the Basic allowance disappears faster than you expect once you start iterating.

The sourcing on those Midjourney figures needs flagging again, for the same reason as in August. I tried midjourney.com, docs.midjourney.com and the plans page on November 23, 2026 and all three returned 403 to an automated request. That is a bot wall, not a dead company, but it does mean I am reporting numbers that are consistent across several independent trackers rather than numbers I read off the vendor's page. Check before you pay.

So the money question is now genuinely three-sided. Zero dollars with ads during generation. Ten dollars with no ads and no assistant. Twenty dollars with no ads, an assistant, and the thinking mode.

## Which one to pick now

If you want to find out whether AI images are useful to you at all, start on ChatGPT's free tier and accept the ads. Knowing whether this suits your work is worth more than the irritation, and it costs nothing to learn.

If you generate images most days and they are not the thing you sell, Plus at $20 is the sane answer, especially if you already pay for it. Blog covers, social graphics, slide images, mockups. Adding a $30 Midjourney subscription on top of a ChatGPT one you already have is hard to justify for that work.

I would skip Go at $8 for image work specifically. You pay, you still get ads, and you cannot buy your way out of them without going to $20. The $10 Midjourney plan is a better use of money very near that price if images are why you are subscribing.

If the images are the product, Midjourney. Concept art, editorial work, anything where a client is paying for how it looks rather than what it depicts. Its aesthetic tuning is a deliberate product decision rather than a temporary lead, so I expect it holds.

If you need readable text inside the image, test both this week rather than trusting any comparison including this one.

If you need guaranteed commercial safety, neither is the automatic answer. Licensing is where AI image tools get people into trouble, and self-hosted open models are worse rather than better on this despite feeling freer. I go through the licence terms model by model in [Midjourney vs Stable Diffusion](/blog/midjourney-vs-stable-diffusion).

## If you are new to Midjourney

The Discord workflow put people off and the web app has largely fixed that, but it is still a dedicated tool with its own prompt language rather than something you talk to. Atmospheric prompts beat structured ones, and parameters like aspect ratio and stylisation do real work once you understand them.

The [step by step Midjourney guide](/blog/how-to-use-midjourney-beginners) covers setup. One caveat on it and on every other Midjourney tutorial you will find: most were written against v6 or v7, and specific parameter advice ages badly across major versions. Treat the workflow as durable and the exact numbers as suggestions.

## The wider lesson, which is not about images

Two things happened to this comparison in six months. One tool in the title stopped existing, and the free version of the other one started carrying advertising. Neither was a scandal and neither was announced to the people it affects in a way they would notice.

What costs people real work is treating a hosted AI tool as permanent storage. If an output matters, it lives on your own disk, not in a conversation history you do not control. I wrote about the same failure mode in [the ElevenLabs alternatives piece](/blog/best-elevenlabs-alternatives), where two of the tools most often recommended had already gone.

The ads point adds a second habit worth having. When a vendor makes something free, find out what pays for it before you build a workflow on top of it.

## Verdict

This is no longer a $20 versus $30 decision, and it is no longer even a question about image quality.

Midjourney remains the better tool if you are selling how something looks, it has shipped three versions since anyone tested it properly against DALL-E, and it will not advertise at you.

OpenAI's side is cheaper to start, bundled with something you may already pay for, no longer called DALL-E, and at the cheap end it now sells your attention while you wait for your picture. That is a reasonable trade for a lot of people. It is not the same offer the free tier was making six months ago, and I would rather you heard that here than noticed it mid-render.

The thing I would not do is pick either one based on a comparison written against the previous generation of both. Including, until today, this one.
