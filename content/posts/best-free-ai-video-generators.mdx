I went looking for the best free AI video generators and found that most of the tools on everyone's list are not free any more.

Not "free with limits." Not "free tier available." Actually not free, or free in a way that gives you about ten seconds of video and then stops forever.

This is what checking each one produced, on August 17, 2026.

## What stopped being free

**Sora.** Free ChatGPT accounts lost video generation on January 10, 2026. It now requires Plus at $20 a month as a minimum. Reporting puts Plus at around 50 videos a month with a 20 second cap per clip, and Pro at $200 with roughly 500 videos and a 90 second cap.

That is seven months ago, and articles are still telling people to generate video free in ChatGPT.

**Runway.** This is the one worth reading carefully, because Runway's free offer is described accurately on their own pricing page and gets misreported everywhere else. Their words: **125 one-time credits.** Not monthly. Once.

Their Gen-4.5 model costs 60 credits per five seconds of video. So the arithmetic is:

| What you get | Credits | Video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free, one time | 125 | about 10 seconds, total, ever |
| Standard, $12/mo annual | 625/mo | about 52 seconds a month |
| Pro, $28/mo annual | 2,250/mo | about 3 minutes a month |
| Max, $76/mo annual | 9,500/mo | about 13 minutes a month |

Ten seconds is enough to decide whether you like the tool. It is not a free tier in any sense that matters, and calling it one is the single most common error in this category.

**Luma.** In March 2026 Luma launched Luma Agents, an agentic platform wrapped around the same models, with plans at $30, $90 and $300 a month and no free option. The legacy Dream Machine plans reportedly still exist alongside it and keep a free tier. So whether Luma is free depends on which product page you land on, which is not a distinction any older article makes.

## What is still genuinely free

Two tools, on the figures I could find.

**Pika.** Reported at 150 daily credits, clips up to ten seconds on some modes, and no visible watermark on free output. That last part is unusual and it is Pika's real advantage over Kling.

**Kling.** Reported at 66 daily credits, which works out at roughly two to six short clips a day depending on resolution. Capped at five seconds and 720p, with a visible Kling watermark on everything.

Kling's number used to be higher. It is reported to have been cut from 166 daily credits to 66, roughly a 60% reduction, and most pages ranking for Kling's free plan still quote the old figure.

Both reset daily and neither rolls over. Unused credits vanish at midnight, so the practical allowance is what you can use in one sitting, not what accumulates.

## Pika, in practice

Pika is the one I would start with, and the reason is the watermark rather than the credits.

A watermark does not just look unprofessional. It rules out most of the things people actually want AI video for: a product clip, a background loop for a landing page, b-roll cut into a longer edit, anything a client sees. A watermarked clip is a preview of work you cannot use. Pika's free output reportedly does not carry one, which turns its free tier from a demo into something you could genuinely ship.

The reported 150 daily credits go further than Kling's 66, and the ten second ceiling on some modes is double Kling's five. Ten seconds is a meaningful unit in short-form video, because it is long enough to be a complete shot rather than a fragment.

What to be realistic about: credits reset daily and do not accumulate. If you need twenty clips for one project, that is several days of collecting rather than an afternoon, and the model may not stay consistent across sessions. Generated video still struggles with continuity between clips, so cutting five separately generated shots together tends to look like five different worlds unless you are deliberate about prompting.

## Kling, in practice

Kling's free tier is more restricted on every axis: reported 66 daily credits, five second clips, 720p, and a visible watermark on everything.

It is still worth knowing about for two reasons. The output quality at 720p is competitive with tools charging money, and the daily reset means you can accumulate material over a week without paying, provided you can live with the watermark while you evaluate.

The number is the thing to watch. It reportedly dropped from 166 daily credits to 66, and most pages still quote the old figure, which means a lot of people arrive expecting roughly two and a half times what they get. If you are planning around Kling, check what your account actually shows today rather than what an article says, including this one.

## What you can realistically make with a free tier

This is the part most listicles skip, and it decides whether any of this is worth your time.

**A single social clip: yes.** Five to ten seconds of generated video, cut with music and text in a normal editor, is a complete piece of short-form content. Both free tiers here support that daily.

**B-roll for a longer video: yes, slowly.** If you need three or four generated inserts for a ten minute YouTube video, a daily free allowance collects that over a few days. This is probably the highest-value free use, because the generated clips are seasoning rather than the meal.

**A product demo or explainer: no.** These need continuity between shots, specific on-screen content, and usually more than a minute of footage. Free tiers cannot do it and neither can the cheapest paid tiers.

**Anything with a person speaking: no, from these tools.** Use the avatar category below instead. Generated video is poor at lip sync and consistent faces, and the tools built for talking heads solve a different and easier problem.

**Client work: only after reading the licence.** Watermarks aside, free tiers routinely grant narrower rights than paid ones.

The honest summary is that free AI video is for evaluation and for small inserts into work you are producing another way. Anyone telling you a free tier replaces a video production budget is selling something.

## Avatar video is a different category with better free tiers

If what you want is a presenter reading a script rather than a generated scene, the economics change completely.

**Synthesia** is reported at 10 minutes a month with 9 avatars, watermarked. **HeyGen** is reported at 3 videos a month, watermarked, at 720p.

Ten minutes a month is a genuinely useful amount of video. The catch in both cases is the watermark, which makes the output fine for testing, internal use, or deciding whether to pay, and unusable for anything client facing.

The reason these free tiers are more generous is that avatar video is cheaper to produce than generated scenes. The model is compositing a known face onto a known background with synthesised speech, not inventing every frame. Different cost structure, different free tier.

## Think in seconds, not credits

Every tool in this category prices in credits, and no two credits are worth the same thing. This is the same problem the AI coding tools have, where six vendors sell six incompatible units, and it exists for the same reason: it makes comparison impossible.

The way through it is to convert everything into seconds of finished video before you compare anything.

Runway publishes the rate, so the conversion is arithmetic: 60 credits per five seconds on Gen-4.5. Most others do not publish a clean rate, and the cost per generation varies with resolution, duration and model, so you have to generate one clip and watch what your balance does.

That test takes two minutes and tells you more than any comparison table, including this one. Generate one clip at the settings you would actually use, note the credit cost, divide your monthly allowance by it. That is your real capacity.

## The pattern behind all of this

Free AI video collapsed in 2026, and it collapsed faster than free AI images did.

The reason is not complicated. Generating video costs enormously more compute than generating a still, and the gap is not close. A five second clip is not five seconds of one image, it is dozens of frames that have to stay coherent with each other. Free tiers in image generation survived because the marginal cost of one more image is small. In video it is not, so the free tiers either shrank, became one-time trials, or were withdrawn.

Expect this to continue. If you are relying on a free video tier for anything ongoing, plan for it to shrink, and do not build a workflow that breaks when it does. I have written before about [what AI pricing did in 2026](/blog/ai-pricing-changes-2026), and video is the clearest case of the pattern: the headline price stays still or disappears while what sits behind it moves.

**If you need a talking head,** Synthesia's reported 10 minutes a month is the most usable free allowance in this whole piece, watermark accepted.

**If you are producing content weekly,** stop looking at free tiers. Runway Standard at $12 a month billed annually is about 52 seconds of Gen-4.5, and short-form video is usually several short clips cut together rather than one long generation, so 52 seconds goes further than it sounds. That is the honest entry point for regular work.

## What to expect from the output itself

Free tier or paid, the quality ceiling in generated video is lower than the demo reels suggest, and it is worth knowing where before you spend a day on it.

**Short is reliable, long is not.** A five second clip usually holds together. Coherence degrades as duration grows, which is part of why the caps exist and why the paid tiers that offer 90 seconds are selling something harder than nine times a ten second clip.

**Hands, text and faces remain difficult.** The same weaknesses that image generators have are worse in motion, because an error that would be a static oddity becomes a visible glitch across frames. Anything with readable text on screen is best added afterwards in an editor rather than generated.

**Consistency between clips is the real limitation.** You can generate two good five-second shots and find they look like different productions. Prompting the same style, lighting and subject helps and does not solve it. This is the single biggest reason free tiers work better for inserts than for whole videos.

**Motion is where these tools earn their keep.** Camera movement, particle effects, atmospheric shots, abstract backgrounds. Generated video is genuinely good at things that would be expensive to film and forgiving of small inconsistencies. It is weakest at exactly the things a phone camera does easily.

That last point is the useful one for deciding what to attempt. Ask whether the shot would be hard to film. If it would be easy, film it. If it would need a drone, a studio, or a visual effects budget, that is where a ten second generation earns its place.

## One warning about licensing

Watermarks are the visible restriction. The invisible one is what rights you get.

Free tiers routinely grant narrower usage rights than paid ones, and some make your output public by default. That is not hypothetical: I found exactly that pattern when checking free image tools, where one popular option makes free-tier generations public and retains reuse rights, which most articles recommending it never mention. The same class of term exists in video.

Before you use any free-tier output in client work, read what you actually own. It takes five minutes and it is the difference between a free tool and an expensive mistake. The equivalent checks for still images are in [best free AI image generators](/blog/best-free-ai-image-generators).

There are two mainstream AI video tools with a real recurring free tier in August 2026: Pika and Kling. Everything else is either a one-time trial dressed as a free plan, an avatar tool in a different category, or paid.

That is a much shorter list than any article promising ten free AI video generators, and it is the one that survives checking.
