Notion AI costs $20 per member per month in 2026, and that sentence is doing a lot of quiet work. You are not buying a $20 AI tool. You are buying a $20 workspace tier that happens to include AI, because Notion killed the standalone AI add-on and moved the whole thing behind the Business plan.

That distinction is the entire review. If you already pay for Business, the AI is a genuinely good deal and you should turn it on today. If you are a solo user on Plus wondering whether to upgrade, the math is worse than any of the review sites will tell you, and it got worse again in May.

I went through Notion's own pricing pages, help documentation, and product announcements rather than the recycled listicles, because most of what ranks for this topic quotes an add-on price that has not existed since 2025.

## The pricing reset nobody explains properly

Here is what changed, in order.

Notion used to sell AI the obvious way: pick any plan, add AI for roughly $10 per member per month. That is the model almost every "best AI tools" post still describes, including a few of my own that I need to go fix.

Notion retired that add-on. Full AI access now lives on Business and Enterprise only. Notion's help center puts it without any hedging: "Notion AI is only available on Business and Enterprise Plans. Users on the Free and Plus Plans get a limited number of complimentary AI responses so they can try Notion AI features out."

Read that second sentence again, because the wording is deliberate. Complimentary responses are a trial, not an allowance. They do not refill monthly. Once your workspace burns through them, the AI features stay locked until somebody upgrades. Notion pointedly does not publish the number on that help page. Third-party guides converge on 20 responses, one time, per workspace, and every "try Notion AI free" headline you have seen is built on that footnote.

So the current shape is:

| Plan       | Price per member/month | What you get from AI                                                            |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Free       | $0                     | A one-time batch of complimentary responses                                     |
| Plus       | $10                    | The same one-time batch, nothing more                                           |
| Business   | $20                    | Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes, Enterprise Search (beta), limited Research mode |
| Enterprise | Custom                 | All of the above, full Research mode, zero data retention                       |

Notion advertises up to 20 percent off for yearly billing, so check the toggle at checkout before you assume the monthly figure is what you will pay annually.

The upgrade also drags along everything else on Business: SAML SSO, private team spaces, advanced permissions, bulk PDF export. If you are a team of eight who wanted those anyway, great, the AI arrives as a bonus. If you are one person who wanted a smart notes assistant, you are paying for an enterprise permissions system you will never open.

## Then May happened

In February 2026, Notion announced Custom Agents. These are the interesting part of the product: agents that run on a schedule or a trigger, read your workspace, call out to Slack, Mail, Calendar, Figma, Linear, or a custom MCP server, and do multi-step work without a human clicking anything. Answer a recurring question from the team wiki. Route an incoming request into the right database and assign it. Compile a weekly report from four sources and post it.

They were free to use on Business and Enterprise through May 3, 2026. On May 4, they moved to usage-based pricing through a new currency called Notion credits.

Credits cost $10 per 1,000. They are an add-on to Business and Enterprise, billed alongside your subscription. Notion's documentation is clear about the mechanics, and the mechanics matter more than the headline price:

Credits reset monthly and unused credits do not roll over. If you buy 1,000 and use 300, the other 700 evaporate. Consumption depends on how much information the agent reads, how many tools it calls in one run, how many steps it takes, and which model it uses, with more capable models costing more. Admins get in-app and email warnings at 80 percent and 100 percent of usage. When the credits run out, agents pause automatically until the next reset or until somebody buys more. Notion credits have no cash value. And downgrading the workspace to Free or Plus switches your existing Custom Agents off entirely.

That last one deserves a second of thought if you are about to spend a weekend building agents on a plan you might not keep.

## The credit math, run properly

Nobody publishes this part, so here it is.

Notion's own examples put a Custom Agent at roughly 30 to 60 runs per 1,000 credits, depending on the workflow. At $10 per 1,000 credits, that is about 17 to 33 cents per run.

Take the most boring agent anyone actually wants: a morning summary that reads yesterday's updates across a few databases and posts a digest. Run it once every weekday and you are at roughly 22 runs a month. Call it $4 to $7 in credits. Fine. Nobody is going to cancel over that.

Now make it useful. Run it every morning including weekends, add a second agent that triages incoming requests, add a third that compiles a weekly report from Slack and Linear. You are somewhere around 90 to 120 runs a month across three agents, several of them heavier than the example workflows because they touch more tools. That is a plausible $30 to $50 a month in credits on top of your seats.

The numbers going around from teams who ran agents hard during the free window are worse. Reported burn rates include a single scheduled agent hitting roughly 3,000 credits in a month, about $30, and one team discovering they had consumed 150,000 credits in a month, which is $1,500 at the current rate. I cannot verify individual invoices, and I would treat any specific horror story as an anecdote rather than a benchmark. But the shape of the risk is real and it comes straight from Notion's own list of consumption factors: an agent that reads a lot, calls a lot of tools, and takes a lot of steps costs a lot per run, and you find out afterwards.

So the honest total cost of ownership for a five-person team that actually uses agents looks like $100 a month in seats plus somewhere between $20 and $80 in credits, with the top end open-ended until you set a budget and watch it. That is a different product from the $20 tool the marketing implies.

The mitigation is straightforward and Notion built it: the 80 percent warning, the automatic pause, and the fact that credits are purchased in fixed blocks rather than metered against your card without limit. You will not wake up to a surprise $1,500 charge unless somebody kept clicking buy. But you can absolutely wake up to your agents paused on the 19th of the month.

## What Business actually gets you

Set the money aside for a moment. The product is good.

Notion Agent is the in-workspace assistant, and it does the thing that makes workspace AI worth having: it reads your stuff. Ask what did we decide about the pricing page in March and it searches your pages, finds the meeting note, and answers with a link. A general model cannot do that, no matter how much smarter it is, because it has never seen your March meeting note. Notion has said the agent can work autonomously for extended stretches on multi-step tasks, updating trackers and drafting summaries across many pages in one go.

AI Meeting Notes records a meeting live inside Notion, transcribes it, and produces a summary attached to the page. When it works, it removes the worst part of meetings.

Enterprise Search, still in beta, searches across your connected tools rather than just Notion, and exposes model selection so you can switch between the underlying models for a given query.

Research mode is the deep-research equivalent, gated by tier: limited on Business, full on Enterprise. If you were hoping to run long multi-source research on a Business seat, check the current limits before you build a workflow around it.

AI autofill fills database properties automatically, which sounds minor and is not. A database of 200 customer interviews where a column summarizes each one in a sentence is a genuinely different artifact from 200 raw pages.

Image generation exists and is in beta, capped at 10 generations or edits per 24 hours and 30 per 30 days. Those are real numbers from Notion's FAQ and they are low enough that you should not plan any workflow around them.

On models, Notion routes to OpenAI and Anthropic and mostly chooses for you. It does not publish a fixed version list, so treat any review that names a specific model version as speculation. Business workspaces get a 30-day retention window with the LLM providers. Enterprise gets zero data retention. If your workspace holds client material under an NDA, that difference is the reason to look at Enterprise, not the extra features.

## Where it falls down

Four things, and only one of them is about money.

Meeting Notes does not identify speakers. This is the complaint that comes up in nearly every hands-on review, and it is a real limitation rather than a nitpick. A transcript that cannot tell you who committed to what is a record of a conversation, not minutes of a meeting. For a solo call or a recorded talk it is fine. For a six-person planning session it means you still take notes.

It also will not ingest a recording you already have. Notion records live; it does not accept a Zoom .mp4, a Meet recording, a Teams file, or a podcast you dropped on your desktop. Reviewers testing it in 2026 consistently flag this, and it rules out the most common real workflow, which is "my meeting happened somewhere else, summarize it." Language coverage outside English is also weaker, and accents and technical vocabulary degrade the transcript quickly.

The general model underneath is weaker than what you get from a dedicated assistant. Notion AI writes shorter, flatter drafts than ChatGPT or Claude will. It is a retrieval and summarization layer with writing bolted on, and it shows the moment you ask for something creative or structurally complex. That is not a scandal. It is what happens when the product's job is to know your workspace rather than to be the best writer available.

And the free tier is misleading in a way that annoys me. A one-time batch of complimentary responses, with no published number, presented under language that reads like a trial of the product, is not enough to evaluate anything. You will burn it on three curiosity prompts and a retry, because clicking "try again" counts as another response. If you want to actually assess Notion AI before committing your team, budget one month of Business for one person and test it properly.

## Building a Custom Agent without wrecking the budget

If you do go in, the setup order matters more than the prompt you write. Notion's consumption factors tell you exactly which levers move the bill, so build against them.

Scope the data before you scope the task. An agent pointed at your entire workspace reads your entire workspace on every run, and reading is the first thing Notion lists as a credit driver. Point it at one database, or at one teamspace, and the same output costs a fraction. This is the single biggest difference between a cheap agent and an expensive one, and it is invisible until the invoice.

Cut the number of tools per run. An agent that checks Notion, then Slack, then Linear, then Calendar makes four round trips, and each connector call is another step. Three narrow agents that each touch one system usually cost less than one agent that touches all of them, and they are easier to debug when one starts returning nonsense.

Pick the cheaper model where the task is mechanical. Notion states outright that more capable models consume more credits. A daily digest that concatenates and summarizes does not need the strongest model available. A weekly analysis that has to reason across conflicting project updates might.

Run it less often than your instinct says. Hourly feels responsive and costs roughly 30 times what a daily run costs for output most people read once. Start daily. Move to hourly only if somebody complains about latency, which they usually will not.

Watch the first cycle before you build the second agent. You get an 80 percent warning and an automatic pause, which means the first month is effectively a free measurement of your own workflow. Use it. Build one agent, let it run four weeks, read the actual consumption, then extrapolate. Everyone who reported a shocking bill built five agents in week one and read the number in week five.

One more practical note: because credits do not roll over, buying a large block to be safe is the wrong instinct. Buy close to your measured usage and top up, rather than pre-purchasing a buffer that expires monthly.

## The comparison that actually matters

Every review sets this up as Notion AI vs ChatGPT, which is the wrong fight. The real question is what your $20 does.

ChatGPT Plus at $20 buys one person a strong general model for writing, analysis, research, and code, on any topic, with no setup. If you want a deeper read on that tradeoff I went through it in the [ChatGPT Plus review](/blog/chatgpt-plus-review), and the [Claude vs ChatGPT comparison](/blog/claude-vs-chatgpt) covers the other obvious contender.

Notion AI at $20 per member buys a weaker general model that has read everything your team has written. That is the whole value proposition and it is not a small one. The questions it answers are the ones a general model structurally cannot: what did we decide, where is that document, what is the status across these forty projects, summarize the last six client calls.

Which means the tools do not compete. They stack. Most people I would recommend Notion AI to are already paying for a general assistant and will keep paying for it, so the honest framing for a solo user is $40 a month, not $20. That is worth saying out loud because no review site says it.

For a team, the math flips. Five people already on Business are paying nothing extra for AI, and one shared workspace brain that answers questions from the company wiki saves more hours than five individual chat subscriptions do. Teams are who this product is for now, and the pricing change was Notion admitting it.

## Who should buy it

Buy it if your team is already on Notion Business or heading there anyway. The AI is included, it is good, and not turning it on is leaving money on the table.

Buy it if your organizational knowledge genuinely lives in Notion and people keep asking each other questions the wiki already answers. That is the exact problem Notion Agent solves and it solves it better than any bolt-on search tool.

Buy it if you run recurring operational work that is currently a human copying things between Notion, Slack, and Linear every Monday. Custom Agents handle that, and even at 33 cents a run the arithmetic against a person's Monday morning is not close. Set a credit budget first.

Do not buy it if you are a solo user on Plus who mostly wants writing help. Upgrade to Business only if you want the Business features. Otherwise put the $20 into ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro and keep Notion as a notes app, which it is very good at for free. The [freelancer tools guide](/blog/best-ai-tools-for-freelancers) and the [student tools guide](/blog/best-ai-tools-for-students) both have cheaper picks for that job.

Do not buy it for meetings. If meeting notes are the reason you are considering this, a dedicated transcription tool with speaker labels and file import will serve you better for less.

Do not build a business process on Custom Agents until you have watched a full billing cycle of credit consumption. Everything about the credit system is knowable in advance except how much your specific agents cost, and that only shows up in use.

## Verdict

Notion AI in 2026 is a strong product wrapped in pricing that quietly moved the goalposts twice in a year. The workspace-aware retrieval is the real thing, Custom Agents are more capable than the automations most teams have today, and for a team already on Business the whole package arrives at no extra cost.

The problem is who pays. Killing the add-on pushed solo users and small teams from a $20 total into a $20-per-seat tier they did not need, and the May credit switch turned the most compelling feature into a metered line item with no ceiling until you set one.

I would rate it 7 out of 10, with the caveat that the score swings hard on one question. If Notion is where your work lives, it is closer to a 9. If Notion is one of six tools you use, it is closer to a 5, and your $20 belongs somewhere else.

Check Notion's current [pricing page](https://www.notion.com/pricing) before you commit. This product's pricing has changed twice in eighteen months and I would not assume today's numbers are permanent.
