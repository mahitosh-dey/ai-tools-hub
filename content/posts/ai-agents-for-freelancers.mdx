Every agent tool aimed at freelancers prices itself the same way: a number on a page, a credit allowance you cannot convert into hours, and a free tier that may or may not come back next month.

So I read the pricing pages of six of them and wrote down what they actually charge. The pattern that came out is simpler than I expected.

**Everything a solo freelancer can genuinely use costs $20. Everything sold as "AI agents for business" starts at $50.** The jump has very little to do with capability and quite a lot to do with who the sales page is written for.

## The six, priced from their own pages

| Tool | Free tier | Cheapest paid | Read on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Claude** | yes, renews | $17/mo annual, $20 monthly | 5 Oct 2026 |
| **Cursor** | Hobby, renews | $20/mo | 5 Oct 2026 |
| **Devin Desktop** | light quota, renews | $20/mo Pro | 5 Oct 2026 |
| **Strawberry** | 500 credits/mo, renews | $20/mo Intern | 26 Oct 2026 |
| **n8n** | self-host, free | **€20**/mo annual | 27 Oct 2026 |
| **Lindy** | **7-day trial only** | **$49.99/mo** | 27 Oct 2026 |

Note the currency on n8n. It prices in euros, not dollars, which is the kind of detail that quietly breaks a budget spreadsheet.

## If you write code, this is already solved

Three tools, all $20, all with free tiers that renew.

**Cursor** at $20 gives you an editor that edits across files rather than suggesting one line at a time. The free Hobby tier is real and renews.

**Devin Desktop** at $20 Pro, with a free tier described by its own page as a "light quota". This is the product formerly called Windsurf, which Cognition folded into its Devin line. Both windsurf.com and codeium.com now redirect to devin.ai/desktop, which is the clearest acquisition signal you will see.

**Claude** at $17 a month on annual billing or $20 monthly, with Claude Code for terminal work included in the subscription.

A developer freelancer picking any one of these is making a preference decision, not a budget one. That is unusual in this category and worth noticing before you read the next section.

## If you do not write code, the price triples

This is where the category gets expensive, and where the marketing gets loudest.

**Strawberry** is the one that breaks the pattern. Agents run in your own browser using the tabs and logins you already have, for things like researching leads, filling a spreadsheet or drafting replies. Free tier is 500 credits a month and **it renews**, which is rarer than it sounds. Intern is $20 for 6,000 credits, then $85 and $250 for the tiers above.

I have not tested it. It appears on this site in a [costs table](/blog/best-ai-for-small-business) as a price rather than a recommendation, after the company asked to be included and accepted that caveat. They offered free credits so I could test it properly and I declined, because a price row does not need testing and comped access would have to be disclosed forever.

**Lindy** is the clearest example of the $50 wall.

Its free offer is **$50 in credits, no card required, lasting 7 days**. That is a trial wearing the word free. When it ends the cheapest plan is **$49.99 a month for 5,000 credits**, with extra credits at $10 per 1,000. Its own FAQ says every active user is billed starting at $49.99 for 5,000 credits each, which reads as per seat rather than per workspace.

Nothing is wrong with charging $50. What is wrong is the word "free" sitting next to a seven-day clock, and I would not have known without reading the fine print under the button.

**Manus** targets the same audience. I cannot tell you what it costs, which is the next section.

## If you want to automate the admin

**n8n** is the one genuinely free option here, with a condition attached.

The Community Edition is on GitHub and runs on your own machine. If you can deploy a container, this costs nothing and does not meter you. If you cannot, the cheapest cloud plan is **€20 a month billed annually** for 2,500 workflow executions, then €50 for 10,000.

Its Business tier is €667 a month and self-hosted only, which is a useful reminder of who most of this category is actually built for.

The honest trade is your time against their hosting. If a weekend of setup saves you €240 a year, that is a good rate for a freelancer. If it costs you a weekend and then breaks silently in March, it is not.

## What I could not price, and why

**Manus and Gumloop both render their pricing in the browser rather than in the page source.**

Open either in a normal browser and the numbers appear. Fetch the same URL automatically and you get the page title and nothing else. Manus returns a heading, a tagline and some navigation links. Gumloop returns a title and a list of documentation links.

This is not unusual any more. Three other tools hit the same wall on this site this month.

What it means practically: **any comparison article quoting Manus or Gumloop prices either opened a browser and typed them out, or copied someone who did.** Those two are not the same thing, and only one of them is checkable.

So there are no Manus or Gumloop figures in the table above. A gap is more useful than a number I cannot stand behind.

## The thing worth understanding before you pay anything

Agents are sold on the promise of delegation and priced on the assumption of volume.

Every tool here meters you in credits. None of them will tell you how many credits your actual work consumes, because they genuinely do not know: it depends on how much context each task drags along. Strawberry publishes a conversion, Lindy publishes credit tiers, n8n counts workflow executions, and none of those translate into "how many client emails can I draft this month".

Which leads to the only test that has worked for me. **Use the free tier for a month and count how many times you open it without being reminded to.**

If the answer is most days, the $20 is irrelevant and you should pay it. If the answer is twice, you have discovered something worth more than the subscription you did not buy.

Most people discover it is twice. That is not a failure of the tool. It is a freelancer finding out that the work they get paid for is the judgement, not the typing, and agents are extremely good at the typing.

## What I would actually do

**Already paying for Claude or Cursor?** You have an agent. Use it for a month before buying another one.

**Not technical, want to try the category?** Strawberry's free tier renews monthly, which means you can evaluate it properly rather than against a seven-day clock.

**Want automation and can run a container?** n8n self-hosted, and nothing else until it breaks.

**Tempted by a $50 platform?** Wait until a free tier has proven you delegate work at all. The platforms are not overpriced for the teams they are built for. You are probably not that team yet.
