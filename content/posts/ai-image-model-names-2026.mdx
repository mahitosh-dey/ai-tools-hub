Google currently offers five image generation models. They are called Nano Banana, Nano Banana Pro, Nano Banana 2, Nano Banana 2 Lite and Nano Banana 2.1.

I am not being flippant. Those are the names in Google's own documentation, and each one sits beside a completely different formal model ID that is what you actually type into an API call.

I went looking for the current state of AI image generation because three posts on this site were written in August and the field has turned over since. What I found was less about which tool makes better pictures and more about the fact that the basic questions, what is this model called and what does one image cost, have both stopped having straightforward answers.

## The five Nano Bananas

Here is exactly how Google's image generation documentation pairs them, read on **23 November 2026**.

| What Google calls it | What you actually call | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Nano Banana 2.1 | `gemini-nano-banana-2.1` | New |
| Nano Banana 2 | `gemini-3.1-flash-image` | Stable |
| Nano Banana 2 Lite | `gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image` | Stable |
| Nano Banana Pro | `gemini-3-pro-image` | Stable |
| Nano Banana | `gemini-2.5-flash-image` | Older |

Look at the middle column. Only one of the five has "nano banana" in its actual model ID. The rest are Gemini models with a fruit name attached for the public.

That is not a scandal, it is just branding. But it means the name people write about and the name on your invoice are different strings, and an article can be entirely correct about one while telling you nothing about the other.

## The names do not tell you the order

This is the part that actually costs you something.

Ranked by the names alone, you would guess Pro is the best one. You would probably guess 2 is newer than Pro, since 2 sounds like a generation and Pro sounds like a tier. You cannot both be right, and the documentation does not settle it.

I went looking specifically for this. Google's pages never rank Nano Banana Pro against Nano Banana 2, never say which arrived first, and list all four under a single Gemini 3 heading with no ordering explained.

What Google does say, plainly and to its credit, is which one to pick: **"Recommended for all new projects to use Nano Banana 2.1."**

So the answer to "Pro or 2" is neither. The one you want is the one whose name suggests the smallest increment.

There is a second instruction worth having if you are on the original. For anyone still calling plain Nano Banana, the documentation says **"Recommended migration path is Nano Banana 2 Lite for higher quality, faster speeds, and lower pricing."** A model called Lite, offered as an upgrade on quality. The naming is doing nobody any favours.

## OpenAI will not tell you what one image costs

Now the finding that has a practical consequence.

Image generation used to be priced per picture. You looked up a number, multiplied it by how many images you needed, and had your answer. That is how most people still think about it, and it is how most articles still describe it.

OpenAI's image pricing section is headed **"Prices per 1M tokens unless noted."**

Every image model is billed in tokens. Here is the current table, read the same day:

| Model | Image input | Image output | Cached |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt-image-2.6-sunburst` | $8.00 | $30.00 | $2.00 |
| `gpt-image-2.6-flare` | $8.00 | $30.00 | $2.00 |
| `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` | $8.00 | $30.00 | $2.00 |
| `gpt-image-2.5-flare` | $8.00 | $30.00 | $2.00 |
| `gpt-image-2` | $8.00 | $30.00 | $2.00 |
| `gpt-image-1.5` | $8.00 | $32.00 | $2.00 |
| `gpt-image-1-mini` | $2.50 | $8.00 | $0.25 |
| `chatgpt-image-latest` | $8.00 | $32.00 | $2.00 |

All per million tokens. **There is no per-image price anywhere on the page.** Where one would be, the page says to use "the calculator in the image generation guide" instead.

I want to be fair about why this happened. These models take images as input, edit them, and return them at different sizes, so a single flat price per picture genuinely does not describe the product any more. Token pricing is more honest about that.

But the consequence is real. You can no longer answer "what will a thousand images cost me" by reading a page. You have to model it. And every article that quotes a per-image figure for an OpenAI model is quoting something the vendor no longer publishes.

## Eight models on one page, four on the other

While I was there I counted.

OpenAI's **pricing** page lists eight image models. Its **models** page lists four: the 2.6 and 2.5 pairs only. The other four, including `gpt-image-2` and `chatgpt-image-latest`, can be billed to you but do not appear on the page that explains what the models are.

There is a smaller oddity in the four that do appear. The models page describes GPT-Image-2.5 Sunburst as **"Our most capable model for image generation and editing"**, on the same page that lists GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst above it. Either the label did not move when 2.6 shipped, or 2.6 is not more capable, and the page does not say which.

I found the same shape of gap at GitHub when I [compared seven vendors' pricing pages against their own documentation](/blog/ai-pricing-page-vs-docs-audit). The page that takes your money and the page that explains the product are maintained by different people on different schedules, and the gap between them is where your assumptions go wrong.

## OpenAI's names are stranger, and better explained

It would be easy to make this a piece about Google, so here is the comparison that stopped me doing that.

OpenAI's image models are called Sunburst and Flare. As names those are, if anything, more opaque than a fruit: nothing about either word suggests what it does or which is bigger.

But the models page says what they are for, in one line each:

| Model | What OpenAI says it is for |
|---|---|
| GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst | "Image generation and editing for detailed visual work" |
| GPT-Image-2.6 Flare | "Image generation and editing for fast iteration" |
| GPT-Image-2.5 Sunburst | "Our most capable model for image generation and editing" |
| GPT-Image-2.5 Flare | "Fast, high-quality everyday image generation" |

Read those and the system is obvious within seconds. **Sunburst is the careful one, Flare is the fast one**, and the number in front is the generation. Two opaque words, immediately decoded, because the documentation does the one job documentation exists to do.

That is exactly what Google's pages do not do for Pro against 2. Google's names are friendlier and its documentation leaves you guessing about the ordering. OpenAI's names mean nothing on sight and its documentation tells you what you need in a sentence.

The lesson is not that one vendor is better at naming. It is that the name is never the information. The sentence next to the name is.

## DALL-E is not deprecated, it is absent

I searched the full text of both OpenAI pages for the string "DALL". It does not appear on either.

Not listed as retiring, not marked legacy, not given a sunset date. Simply gone, replaced by the `gpt-image` line.

When I wrote about [Midjourney against DALL-E 3](/blog/midjourney-vs-dalle3) the story was that DALL-E was on its way out. It has arrived. Anything still comparing those two as live options is comparing one product against a name.

Google has done the same thing to its old line. Its models page lists **"Imagen 4 (Shut down)"**, describing it as a "Text-to-image model featuring fast and ultra-fast generation (shut down)". At least that one says so on the page.

## The tool I could not check

Midjourney returned **403** on both its pricing page and its documentation.

A 403 is a server declining to talk to an automated request. It is not evidence about the product, the company or the prices, and I have not treated it as any.

It does leave an odd situation. The most written-about image generator in the world is the one I can verify least. Every current figure you read about Midjourney pricing, on this site included, rests on a person having opened it in a browser rather than on a check anyone can repeat. That is worth knowing about the sourcing of the whole category, not just mine.

## What I would actually do

**On Google, use Nano Banana 2.1.** Not because I have tested it against the others, but because Google says so in its own documentation and that is better evidence than a ranking in an article.

**On OpenAI, do not budget from a per-image figure you read somewhere.** It is not a number the vendor publishes any more. Run a small batch, read the actual billing, and extrapolate from that.

**Write down the model ID, not the nickname.** `gemini-3-pro-image` means one thing. "Nano Banana Pro" means that today and may mean something adjacent in three months.

**Check the date on anything you read about this category, including this.** Both of these vendors turned over their entire image lineup since August. When I audited articles ranking for the best coding model, [six of the seven I could read recommended a model that had already been replaced](/blog/ai-model-articles-stale-audit), and image models are moving at least as fast.

## Sourcing, and what I could not confirm

Google's model names, model IDs, status badges and recommendation text come from the image generation and models pages at aistudio.google.com, read on **23 November 2026** and quoted from the page text.

OpenAI's image prices, the "Prices per 1M tokens" heading, the eight-against-four count and the absence of DALL-E come from the pricing and models pages at developers.openai.com, read the same day.

Three things I could not stand up, stated rather than quietly dropped:

**Google's image prices are not in this post.** Their pricing page runs to over 100,000 characters and I could only read the first portion as actual page text. Figures exist for the rest, but they reached me through a machine summary rather than the page, and that is not good enough to publish as a price.

**A shutdown date for the original Nano Banana.** I saw one in that same summary and could not confirm it on any page I read properly, so it is not here.

**Midjourney, at all.** Both URLs returned 403.
