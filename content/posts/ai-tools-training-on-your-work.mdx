Gamma's help page says two things on the same screen.

For individuals on Free, Plus, Pro and Ultra: "Data is allowed to be used to train AI models." For Business and Enterprise accounts, the same data is "automatically excluded" and the setting is locked so nobody can switch it back on.

Same product. Same models. Opposite default. The only variable is what you pay.

I spent today reading the privacy policies, terms and help pages of 21 AI tools to find out how common that is. It is very common.

## The one question I asked

For each tool: **can a reader find out whether their own work is being used to train the model, without logging in and without emailing support?**

Not "is it possible in theory." Not "what does the company say about security." Just: does the page tell you what is happening to your work right now.

Of the 21, I can now read 20. When I first ran this on 2 November, OpenAI and Midjourney both returned 403 from every page I tried, so ChatGPT, the most used tool on the list, was missing from every count. OpenAI's pages answer again, and I read them on 25 November. Midjourney was re-tested the same day and still returns 403, so it stays out.

That difference is worth a sentence of its own. Two bot walls looked identical three weeks ago. One was a phase and one has held. There is no way to tell which is which from the status code, which is the argument for re-testing rather than carrying a refusal forward as a fact.

**Nine of the twenty answer the question. Eleven do not.**

## The nine that answer

| Tool | What the page actually says |
|---|---|
| **Grammarly** | "Product Improvement and Training is on for individual users by default" |
| **Gamma** | "Default setting: Individuals (Free, Plus, Pro, and Ultra): Data is allowed to be used to train AI models" |
| **Microsoft Copilot** | trains "except for certain categories of users or users who have opted out" |
| **DeepL** | free version is used "to train and improve our neural networks and algorithms"; Pro texts "will not be used" |
| **Notion** | "By default, your customer data is not used to train models" |
| **Cursor** | "We do not use Inputs or Suggestions to train our models" |
| **Zoom** | "Zoom does not use any of your audio, video, chat, screen sharing, attachments" to train its or third-party models |
| **Adobe** | "Adobe does not analyze your content to train generative AI models, unless you choose to submit content to the Adobe Stock marketplace" |
| **OpenAI** | "On personal workspaces with ChatGPT Free, Plus, or Pro, data sharing is enabled by default" |

When I wrote this with eight entries I noted that four of them were a no, and concluded that the companies which do not train on you tend to say so plainly. Adding OpenAI sharpens that rather than breaking it. The count is now five yes and four no, and the single clearest sentence in the whole audit belongs to a company admitting it trains by default.

So the signal is not that a plain answer means good news. It is that a plain answer means someone decided to write one. Four companies chose to say no and one chose to say yes, and all five are easier to deal with than the eleven below.

## The eleven that do not

Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Canva, Jasper, ElevenLabs, GitHub Copilot, Otter.ai, Descript, Runway and Suno.

Most of them are not hiding anything. They simply stop one sentence short. Runway's terms say "Inputs and Outputs may be used by the Company to train and improve its AI models." May. Suno says its data is used "to train and enhance the models that power our Services." Otter.ai describes "training our proprietary AI technology on de-identified audio recordings and on transcriptions." All true, none of it tells you whether it is happening to your account.

Gemini is the sharpest example. Google states clearly that with the Keep Activity setting on, "Google uses your activity to provide, develop, and improve its services (including training generative AI models)," and that with it off, future chats "won't be used to train our AI models." Both halves are documented. What Google never says, anywhere on that page, is whether Keep Activity is on or off when you start.

## The part nobody has written down

Seven companies publish a different default for consumers than for business customers, on their own pages, in writing.

| Company | If you are a freelancer | If you are an enterprise |
|---|---|---|
| **Gamma** | on by default | "automatically excluded", setting locked |
| **Grammarly** | "on for individual users by default" | off, "with no toggle visible" |
| **ElevenLabs** | not stated | "By default, we don't train on any data from our Enterprise customers" |
| **Anthropic** | used "unless you opt out" | "By default, we will not use your inputs or outputs from our commercial products" |
| **Descript** | used "unless you have opted out" | Enterprise drives "disabled by default" |
| **DeepL** | free text trains the networks | Pro text "will not be used" |
| **OpenAI** | "data sharing is enabled by default" on Free, Plus and Pro | "By default, OpenAI does not use content from ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, or Edu workspaces to train its models" |

Seven out of seven run the same way round.

This is not a scandal and it is not illegal. Enterprise contracts are negotiated, and the people negotiating them ask this question. The point is narrower and worse: the protection exists, it works, it is already built, and whether you get it depends on which invoice you are on. A freelancer pasting a client's confidential brief into the Free tier and a company pasting the same brief into Enterprise are covered by opposite rules.

If you have ever signed an NDA and then used a free AI tool to do that work, this is the table to read twice.

## Three companies contradict themselves

**Anthropic.** The privacy policy says: "We may use your Inputs and Outputs to train and improve Anthropic AI models, unless you opt out through your account settings." The consumer terms repeat it. Both read as on unless you act.

Anthropic's own consumer help article says the opposite. Training happens if "You choose to allow us to use your chats and coding sessions to improve Claude."

Opt out and choose to allow are opposite defaults. Both pages were live on 2 November 2026.

**Descript.** Its terms say content is used to train "unless you have opted out of such use." Its help article says internal research models use "only data from users who have opted into data sharing." Same contradiction, same day.

**OpenAI**, added 25 November 2026, and this is the sharpest of the three because both sentences are about the same product.

Its developer documentation makes a flat promise: "Your data is your data. As of March 1, 2023, data sent to the OpenAI API is not used to train or improve OpenAI models (unless you explicitly opt in to share data with us)." Its endpoint table marks "Data used for training" as No on every row.

Its help centre, on the same day, says OpenAI "may use API content to train safety classifiers and conduct safety evaluations" for "a limited subset of API customers who do not have modified retention enabled and are not managed by our sales team," and that this "does not require an opt-in to model training."

Read those together. The promise says API data never trains a model without opt-in. The help centre documents a training use that needs no opt-in. Neither sentence is false, and a developer reading the page with the promise on it would never know the other existed, because the promise does not link to it.

In all three cases the answer probably exists inside the product, in a settings screen I would have to log in to see, or on a page the first page does not mention. That is the problem. You should not have to create an account, or guess which of a company's documents is the operative one, to find out what it will do with your work.

### The plan that is not in the policy at all

One more OpenAI oddity, which belongs here rather than in a section of its own. Every training sentence on both OpenAI pages names Free, Plus and Pro. **ChatGPT Go is never named.** It appears in the privacy policies only in connection with advertising.

Go is a plan OpenAI actively sells, lists on its pricing page, and warns may include ads. It does not publish Go's price anywhere I can read either. A tier that is absent from both the price list and the training policy is a strange thing to be selling.

## Six tools use your content and give you no switch

Perplexity, Otter.ai, Runway and Suno describe no training opt-out of any kind on the pages I read. Perplexity's privacy notice does carve out one thing: "We do not use the content of emails to create, train, improve, or fine-tune AI models." Your emails are protected. Your searches and uploads are not mentioned.

Jasper has an opt-out, but only if you live in the EU or UK, and it is not a setting. Its terms say you "submit a request by contacting legal@jasper.ai," after which Jasper "will cease using your Customer Property for model training and improvement."

For DeepL, the opt-out is to buy Pro.

## Read Jasper's trust page slowly

It says: "Your company data and outputs are never used to train third-party LLMs."

Its terms say Jasper uses your input and output "for the purpose of developing and enhancing Jasper's artificial intelligence models."

Both sentences are true at once. The trust page is about other people's models. Jasper's own are a different sentence, in a different document, that the trust page does not link to. Nothing here is false. The word doing the work is "third-party," and it is doing a great deal of work.

## Opting out is not an off switch

Three companies say so themselves.

Anthropic: even after you opt out, material is still used when a conversation is flagged for safety review or when you submit feedback, and feedback conversations are stored "for up to 5 years."

Google: "Your Gemini settings don't control processing of your chats to create anonymized data." And chats already seen by a human reviewer "are retained for up to three years" even after you delete your activity. Turning the setting off stops future chats. It does not reach back.

OpenAI: two carve-outs, both in its own words. "If you choose to provide feedback, the entire conversation associated with that feedback may be used to train our models." And during Daybreak Trusted Access sessions on personal accounts, OpenAI "may use content from your interactions to improve our models even when" the setting is off. It also says archiving a chat "does not change whether it can be used to train OpenAI models," that memory is a separate setting, and that voice mode has its own control. Four switches, one question.

The gap in OpenAI's version is what happens to work already used. The page says only that "we won't use your new conversations to improve our models." Nothing on any page I read says whether opting out reaches backwards, which is the same silence Google fills explicitly and in the other direction.

An opt-out narrows what happens to your work. On the evidence of these pages, it does not stop it.

## Sometimes you cannot read the policy at all

Notion gave the clearest no-training statement in the whole audit. It is on a help-center page with no date on it. Notion's actual privacy policy, at notion.com/privacy, redirects to app.notion.com/privacy and returns **401 Unauthorized**. You need an account to read the terms you are agreeing to.

Eight of the 21 companies block at least one page from automated reading, down from nine now that OpenAI has come off the list. Gamma's privacy policy and terms both returned 403, so everything I know about Gamma comes from a help article.

I re-tested the blocked and broken pages on 25 November, three weeks after the first pass, because a refusal is the kind of finding that decays. Almost nothing moved. Gamma's privacy policy and terms: still 403. Midjourney, both the docs and the main domain: still 403. Notion's privacy policy at notion.com/privacy: still redirects and still returns 401 behind the redirect. Google's Generative AI Additional Terms: still served, still says "Last Modified: August 9, 2023," and still says on its own face that it stopped applying on 22 May 2024, which is now a page that has been obsolete for eighteen months and unedited for over three years.

Only OpenAI changed, and it changed completely, from every page refusing to every page answering.

I did not re-test GitHub's Copilot privacy documentation, because I no longer have the exact URL from the first pass and the responsible-use page I tried is a different document. Its 404, and the "deprecated effective 5 March 2026" marking on the Copilot product terms, are recorded as read on 2 November and not re-confirmed.

## The two minute check, for any tool

**Search the privacy policy for "train". Then search the terms for "train" separately.** The answer often lives in only one of them. Jasper's privacy policy is silent on model training while its terms are explicit. Runway is the same. Reading one and not the other gets you the wrong answer.

**Then search both for "default".** This is the word that separates the eight from the eleven, and almost nobody uses it.

**Check whether the sentence says "third-party".** If a company promises not to train third-party models, it has told you nothing about its own.

**If you cannot find a default stated anywhere, assume you are being trained on.** The four companies that do not train on customer content all say so plainly and early. Silence is not neutral here.

## Sourcing

Twenty-one AI tools. Twenty of them read on 2 November 2026, and OpenAI read on 25 November 2026, when its pages started answering automated requests again. For each I read the privacy policy, the terms of service and the relevant help-center articles on the company's own domain, and asked the same question of every one, so the counts are comparable. Every quotation above came off a company page, not from reporting about it.

**What this update did and did not cover.** It filled the OpenAI hole, which was the largest one, and re-tested every page that had refused or broken on the first pass. It did not re-read the other nineteen policies. Those entries stand as read on 2 November and should be treated as three weeks old, which for this subject is long enough to matter. Where a company has changed its policy since, this post does not know.

**Read and counted (20):** Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Grammarly, Perplexity, Canva, Notion, Jasper, ElevenLabs, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Otter.ai, Descript, Runway, Suno, Adobe, Zoom, Gamma, DeepL, OpenAI.

**Could not be read (1):** Midjourney returned 403 from every page tried, across docs.midjourney.com and midjourney.com, on 2 November and again on 25 November. These are live pages behind a bot wall, not dead ones. Midjourney is excluded from every count rather than guessed at.

**OpenAI, read 25 November 2026.** Four pages: help articles 5722486 and 7730893, the privacy policy, and the ROW privacy policy, plus the developer data guide at developers.openai.com. Worth noting where the answer was and was not. Neither privacy policy states a training default. Both help articles do. A reader who did what this post recommends, and searched the privacy policy for the word default, would have come away with nothing.

**OpenAI's dates cannot be cited.** Its help articles print relative dates only: article 5722486 read "Updated: yesterday" and article 7730893 read "Updated: 2 hours ago". There is no absolute date on either, so there is no version of this post that can tell you which revision I read. The privacy policies do carry real dates, 30 July 2026 for the global one and 6 February 2026 for the ROW one, and those are the two documents that do not answer the question.

**Partial reads.** Gamma's privacy policy and terms both returned 403, so its entry rests on a help article dated 24 September 2026. Notion's privacy policy returned 401, so its entry rests on an undated help page. GitHub's Copilot-specific privacy page returned 404, which is why GitHub Copilot sits in the eleven: the document that would answer the question is gone.

**Policy dates, as published.** Gemini 26 October 2026. Perplexity and Runway 23 October 2026. Cursor 11 October 2026. ElevenLabs 9 October 2026. Anthropic 10 September 2026. Zoom 17 September 2026. Microsoft September 2026. Canva 25 August 2026. Grammarly, now published under Superhuman Platform Inc., 6 July 2026. Suno 23 June 2026. Otter.ai 16 June 2026. Adobe 24 October 2025. Jasper 30 May 2025. Descript 14 April 2025.

Policies change, and the ones above were read on a single day. The method in the two minute check will outlast the table.

One last thing. The clearest, most complete, most plainly worded answer in this entire audit came from Notion, and you cannot read Notion's privacy policy without an account.
