I went looking for the best free AI presentation maker and found something more useful than a ranking. Nearly every free tier in this category is metered per slide and per image rather than per presentation, which means the phrase unlimited presentations on a pricing page can sit directly above an allowance that runs out during your second deck.

That is a different trap from the one in image tools, where a free tier usually gives you a number of pictures a day and the number means what it says. Here the unit is doing the work, and almost nobody converts it into something you can plan around.

So this post does the conversion. It also reports which of these tools still exist, because one of the most recommended names in the category has no website left.

## The unit is the whole story

A presentation is not one generation. It is a layout decision and a block of text for every slide, plus a separate image generation for any slide carrying a picture. Images are the expensive part.

That is why the meter sits where it does. A twenty slide deck with a picture on half the slides can cost a vendor more than a long chat session, so pricing pages advertise presentations while the credits underneath are counted much more finely.

The practical consequence is that two people who each make one presentation can spend completely different amounts. Slide count and image count decide it, not the number of decks.

Keep that in mind every time you see a free tier described in decks rather than credits.

Four of the eight either have no free plan or no product. That is the actual answer to which AI presentation makers are free, and it is not the answer the roundups give.

## Gamma, and what its own documentation says

Gamma is where most people end up, and it does run a genuine free plan rather than a trial. Its pricing page returns a 403 to automated requests, so I could not read the plan prices directly. Its help documentation is open, and it turned out to be more informative than a price would have been.

Three things Gamma states in its own help pages.

Credits are consumed by AI features throughout the product, and generating a deck with Create with AI is metered per slide and per image. Longer generations use more credits, and more advanced models cost more than standard ones. For image generation you see the credit cost of the model before you generate, which is genuinely good design and also tells you the cost varies enough to need a warning.

Free users cannot purchase credits. If you run out on the free plan, the only route forward is a paid plan. Paid users can top up at 1,500 credits for $6.

And there is a rollover cap set at twice your monthly allocation, published as a limit of 2,000 on Plus and 8,000 on Pro. That arithmetic is worth doing, because it means the Plus allocation is 1,000 credits a month and Pro is 4,000. Gamma does not spell those monthly numbers out in the help article, but the cap and the rule together give them to you.

Work that through and the free plan gets easier to reason about. Because the meter runs per slide and per image, the deck you are planning matters more than the number of decks. A ten slide text deck and a ten slide deck with a generated picture on every slide are not the same purchase, and the second one is roughly twice the events being metered. That is the calculation to make before you start, not after.

It also explains a complaint I see constantly, which is people saying Gamma burned their credits faster than expected. If you assume the meter is per presentation, a long illustrated deck will feel like a bug. It is not a bug. It is the unit.

Gamma also notes that credit rates and the actions that consume credits are subject to change. Take that seriously. It is the vendor telling you that any number in any article about them, this one included, has a shelf life.

## SlidesAI, and the word that changes everything

SlidesAI runs a free tier of 120 AI credits.

Per year.

Not per month, which is how almost every other allowance on this page is counted, and how you will read it if you are skimming a comparison table. Its paid tiers use the same annual unit: 600 credits a year at $10 a month, and 1,200 a year at $20.83 a month.

I am not saying this is hidden. It is on their pricing page in plain sight. I am saying that a credit number without its time unit is meaningless, and comparison tables strip the unit constantly. If you take one habit from this post, take that one.

## Tome has no website

Tome was one of the most recommended AI presentation tools for two years. It is gone.

The domain tome.com does not resolve at all. Not a redirect, not a parking page, no DNS record to speak of. The other domain, tome.app, still has DNS pointing at Vercel, but every page returns a deployment not found error, including the homepage and the pricing page. That is what an abandoned deployment looks like from the outside.

I want to be careful about what I am claiming. I am reporting what the domains do today, not announcing a shutdown on the company's behalf, because there was no notice for me to read. What I can tell you is that if you follow a recommendation to Tome right now, you arrive at an error page.

Plenty of best AI presentation tool listicles still rank it. That is the same pattern as the tools that quietly renamed or went paid, and it is the reason I check whether a product exists before I write about whether it is good.

## Two tools that are listed as free and are not

Beautiful.ai appears on free AI presentation maker lists constantly. It has a 14 day free trial of Pro or Team, and a free year for students with a valid .edu email address, which is a genuinely good offer if you are a student. There is no ongoing free plan for anyone else. Pro is $12 a month.

Plus AI is the other one. It runs a 7 day trial that includes 1,000 AI credits and works inside Google Slides and PowerPoint rather than as its own editor, which is a real advantage if your team already lives in those. The plans start at $10 per user a month billed annually, which becomes $15 if you pay monthly, and step up through $20, $30 and $200 tiers with 3,000, 6,000 and unlimited credits.

Plus AI also moved house. The old domain plusdocs.com now redirects to plusai.com. Nothing broke for existing users, but an article still sending you to plusdocs.com was written before the change and never revisited, which tells you something about how carefully the rest of it was checked.

Neither of these is a bad product. They are just not free, and being told they are free wastes your time in a specific way: you build half a deck, then hit a wall on day eight.

## The ones with a real free tier

Chronicle runs a free plan alongside paid tiers that run from around $12 up to $59. Worth a note on the domain, because it is a genuine trap: the presentation tool is at chroniclehq.com. The plain chronicle.com belongs to the higher education publication, an entirely different company. I nearly recorded the wrong one.

Presentations.ai has a free tier with a credit allowance, then paid plans at $20 and $100 a month, billed as $240 and $1,200 annually.

Decktopus is the odd one here. Its pricing page shows no free plan at all, which puts it with Beautiful.ai and Plus AI rather than with the free tools, but it publishes something none of the others do: a credits to slides conversion, describing 750 credits a month as roughly 150 slides. That one sentence is more useful than most of the pricing pages I read today, because it does the conversion for you instead of leaving you to guess.

Visme, Storydoc, Simplified and Genially all still operate. Genially moved its pricing to a plans path rather than a pricing path, which is a small thing that breaks old links and a reliable sign of when an article was last checked.

## What you can realistically make for free

Here is the honest summary, given the metering.

If you need one polished deck, occasionally, a free tier will do it. Gamma's free plan generates a real presentation and you can export it. That is a genuine capability and it costs nothing.

If you need a deck a week, no free tier on this list holds up. You will hit the credit ceiling, and on Gamma's free plan you cannot even buy your way past it without upgrading.

If you need decks with a lot of generated imagery, the free tiers collapse fastest of all, because images are the expensive unit and they are metered separately from slides.

And if you are a student, Beautiful.ai's free year for .edu addresses is the strongest offer in this entire post, and it is the one least likely to be mentioned in a listicle, because it does not fit the free tier framing.

## What to expect from the output itself

Two things that surprised me and are worth setting expectations on.

The layout quality across these tools is genuinely good now. The old complaint that AI decks look like a template with your words dropped in has mostly stopped being true for the leaders. Where they still struggle is dense information: a slide with a table, a chart and a caption tends to come back either sparse or crowded, and fixing it by hand is often faster than re-prompting.

Generated images remain the weakest part, and they are also the part that costs you the most credits. For anything client facing, consider generating the deck without images and adding your own, which saves credits and usually looks better. If you do want AI images, my [best free AI image generators](/blog/best-free-ai-image-generators) piece covers tools with far more generous allowances than any presentation tool bundles.

## How to check any of this yourself

Do not trust the table, including mine. Do this instead.

Make one real deck, the actual thing you need rather than a toy example, and check your credit balance before and after. Multiply out to a month. That number is real and it is yours.

It takes about ten minutes and it survives every pricing change that follows, which matters here more than usual, because Gamma's own documentation says credit rates are subject to change.

## Why this category went this way

Presentation free tiers tightened faster than writing free tiers and for a plain reason, not vendor malice. A chat message is one generation. A deck is dozens, and the image generations inside it are the expensive ones. The economics never supported a generous free tier here the way they did for text.

The same force is visible in video, where free tiers collapsed hardest of all because the cost per frame is brutal. I went through that in [free AI video generators](/blog/best-free-ai-video-generators), and the shape of the answer is the same: the more compute a single output needs, the less free the free tier turns out to be. For the wider pattern across the year, [what AI pricing did in 2026](/blog/ai-pricing-changes-2026) tracks the dated changes.

## The verdict

Two things are worth remembering from this.

Gamma is the strongest free plan in the category, and its meter runs per slide and per image, so plan your deck size rather than your deck count. Check the balance after your first real presentation and you will know exactly what you have.

And read the time unit on every credit number you see. SlidesAI's 120 credits are annual. Someone comparing that against a monthly allowance in a table would conclude the opposite of the truth.

If you already pay for Canva, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, look inside that account before you buy anything here. I could not verify their current AI allowances, so I am not quoting numbers at you, but the cheapest presentation tool is usually the one you are already paying for.
