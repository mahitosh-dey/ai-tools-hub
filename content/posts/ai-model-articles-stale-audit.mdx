I went looking for how stale the AI model advice on the open web actually is, so I picked a query people really search, took the articles ranking for it, and checked every model name against the vendor's own page on the same day.

Ten articles. Three would not load. **Of the seven that did, six recommend a model that has been superseded.**

That is the headline, but it is not the interesting part. The interesting part is that these articles pass the test most people would use to catch them.

## What I did

On **23 November 2026** I took the articles ranking for "best AI model for coding 2026" and tried to read ten of them. Then I read Anthropic's models overview and OpenAI's pricing and models pages the same day, and compared every model name.

Three never loaded. Two returned **404**, meaning they are still being surfaced in search results after being deleted, and one returned **403**.

The two 404s are worth a moment. A search engine is still recommending those pages to people. Whatever advice they contained is now unreachable, un-updatable and un-correctable, and it is still occupying a position someone will click. That is the end state of every article in this audit if nobody maintains it.

For the seven that loaded, here is the top recommendation of each.

| Article | Date it shows | Top pick | Still current |
|---|---|---|---|
| DataCamp | September 2026 | Claude Opus 5.5 | **Yes** |
| daily.dev | none visible | Claude Opus 4.8 | No |
| SSOJet, on dev.to | fact-checked 7 Jun | Claude Opus 4.8 | No |
| Security Boulevard | 7 June 2026 | Claude Opus 4.8 | No |
| dev.to, agentic coding | May | Claude Opus 4.7 | No |
| Builder.io | Updated 21 Apr | Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.7 | No |
| abp.io | 17 April 2026 | GPT-5.4 | No |

**Not one of the seven names today's actual flagship on either side.** OpenAI's is GPT-6.2 Astra and Anthropic's top model is Fable 5.2. The closest anyone gets is DataCamp naming GPT-6 Astra, which OpenAI replaced within the last week.

## The part that surprised me

I expected the prices to be wrong too. They are not.

Every price I spot-checked against the vendor's page on 23 November was **correct**:

| Article's claim | Verified today |
|---|---|
| Opus 4.8 at $5 and $25 | Correct |
| Opus 4.7 at $5 and $25 | Correct |
| Sonnet 4.6 at $3 and $15 | Correct |
| Haiku 4.5 at $1 and $5 | Correct |
| Opus 5.5 at $4 and $20 | Correct |

Five for five.

This happens because vendors do not delete old models when they ship new ones. Anthropic still lists Opus 4.6, 4.7, 4.8 and 5 on its page, all at $5 and $25. OpenAI still prices GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6. The old model stays available at the old price, so the number beside its name stays true long after the recommendation stops being good.

So the obvious way to check an article, glance at a price and see if it matches, **actively tells you the wrong answer**. The stale article passes.

One of these articles makes the point better than I can. The SSOJet piece prints "Starting price: $5 input / $25 output per million tokens (verified 2026-06-07)" next to Claude Opus 4.8. That verification badge is honest, it was true when written, and it is still true today. The model it sits beside has been replaced three times.

## Fourteen model names, one of them current

The top pick is only the headline. The tables underneath are worse.

Across the seven articles I counted the distinct OpenAI and Anthropic models named in their rankings and comparison tables. Here is all of it.

**Anthropic, named across the seven:** Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1, Opus 4.8, Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6, Sonnet 5, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5.

**OpenAI, named across the seven:** GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.5, GPT-5.5-Codex, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.4 Mini.

Fourteen names. **One of them, Claude Opus 5.5, is a model either vendor currently recommends.**

Now the other direction. Anthropic's current four are Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5. OpenAI's are GPT-6.2 Astra, GPT-6.2 Sol, GPT-6.1 Terra and GPT-6 Luna. Of those eight, exactly one appears anywhere in any of these seven articles.

Nobody is wrong about the past. Every one of those fourteen models existed and most are still sold. But a reader arriving at any of these pages, in good faith, looking for what to use this week, sees a menu where thirteen of fourteen options are last season's and nothing on the page says so.

## The one that was right, and the one that deserves credit

**DataCamp got the headline right.** Published in September, it names Claude Opus 5.5 as the model most teams should start with, and that is still Anthropic's recommended default. Worth saying plainly, because an audit that finds everyone guilty usually means the auditor set the bar in a convenient place.

Even so, its table has moved underneath it. It lists Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra, and both have since been replaced.

**Builder.io deserves separate credit, and it is the most useful case here.** That article actively tracks model lifecycles. It tells readers that "Gemini 3 Pro was shut down in March 2026" and that GPT-5.2 Codex was being phased out. It carries a visible "Updated April 21, 2026", which puts it ahead of most of the field on transparency.

And its top picks are two generations out.

That is the real finding. Builder.io did not get lazy. It updated in April, and the models moved in May, then July, then September, then November. **Seven months is longer than a model generation lasts.** No amount of care survives that if the refresh cycle is annual.

## Two sources that are one source

The SSOJet ranking appears twice in my sample: once on dev.to and once on Security Boulevard, with the same ten models in the same order and the same price text.

So of seven articles, six are distinct. If you were reading around and saw that ranking twice, you would reasonably take it as corroboration. It is one piece of work published on two domains.

I have run into this before, with benchmark numbers that appeared across a dozen pages and traced back to nobody. The shape is identical: repetition reading as consensus. I wrote about why I will not print those figures in [my model comparison](/blog/gpt5-vs-claude4), and the reasoning has not changed.

## How fast this actually moves

I can put a number on it, because I keep dated readings.

I read OpenAI's developer documentation on **16 November** and again on **23 November**. Between those two reads, GPT-6.2 Astra appeared and became the stated flagship, and GPT-6 Astra lost its flagship card. A whole generation, inside seven days.

On exactly the same two dates, Anthropic's four current models did not change at all. Same names, same prices, same context windows.

So the rate is not steady. It is not steady between vendors and it is not steady over time, which means a fixed refresh schedule is the wrong instrument. An article refreshed every quarter will be current some quarters and two generations out in others, with no way for the reader to tell which.

## The thirty second check that works

Forget the price. Forget whether the title says 2026. Forget the date on the page, which two thirds of these articles do not show you anyway.

**Open the vendor's own model page and look for the model the article recommends.**

- **In the latest or recommended section:** the article is current.
- **On the page, but further down:** the model is real and still sold, and the article is behind. This is the common case and it is the one every other check misses.
- **Not on the page at all:** the model is retired, and the article is recommending something you cannot buy.

For Anthropic that page is the models overview on platform.claude.com. For OpenAI it is the models page on developers.openai.com. Both list current models at the top and older ones below, which is exactly the distinction you need.

That is it. One page, thirty seconds, and it is the only check in this piece that would have caught all six.

## What I am not claiming

The six stale articles are not recommending broken products. Claude Opus 4.8 is a capable coding model and you can still buy it at the price they quote.

What they are doing is quieter. They do not tell you the same vendor now sells Opus 5.5 at **$4 and $20**, against Opus 4.8 at **$5 and $25**. Follow the old advice and you pay more for a less current model, and nothing in the article tells you there was a decision to make.

I also want to be honest about the limits of this. Seven readable articles is a small sample from one query on one day. I did not evaluate whether any of these models is actually better at coding, because I could not trace the benchmark numbers these articles rely on to a published run, which is the same reason I keep refusing to print a scoreboard. And I checked OpenAI and Anthropic model names against their own pages, but I did not verify the Google, DeepSeek, Kimi or Qwen names to the same standard, so this audit covers the two vendors I could check properly.

This site is not exempt either. The comparison I link above was itself a generation behind until I rewrote it this morning, which is how I came to run this audit in the first place.
