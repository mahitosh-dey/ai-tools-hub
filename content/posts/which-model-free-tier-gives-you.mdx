Every article about free AI tools tells you which tools are free. Almost none tells you which model you get when you use them, and that is now the question that decides whether the free tier is any good.

So I read five vendors' own pages on 16 November 2026 and tried to answer it. Three gave me something. Two publish nothing at all. And one page contradicts itself in a way I had to read four times.

## Google's free card and its paid card name the same model

Start with the one that stopped me.

Google's subscriptions page lays out its plans as cards. Here is what each card says, in the order the page prints it.

| Card | First model bullet | Line at the bottom of the card |
|---|---|---|
| Free, 0 rupees | "Access to our Flash-Lite model" | "Includes Gemini 4 Hydrogen" |
| Google AI Plus, 399 rupees | "Access to Gemini 4 Hydrogen" | "Includes Gemini 4 Argon" |
| Google AI Pro | "Access to Gemini 4 Argon" | "Includes Gemini 4 Carbon" |

Read the Plus card's first selling point. Now read the last line of the Free card. Both of them say Gemini 4 Hydrogen.

The Plus card opens with the words "Everything in free and:" before listing what you get for 399 rupees a month, and the first thing on that list is access to a model the free card has already named.

### What I can and cannot tell you about it

There is a pattern here, and it is worth describing precisely rather than dramatically. Each card's footer line names the model that the next tier up lists as a bullet. Free's footer says Hydrogen and Plus's bullet says Hydrogen. Plus's footer says Argon and Pro's bullet says Argon. Pro's footer says Carbon.

The page never explains what that footer line means. It is not a bullet, not a comparison row, and carries no asterisk or note. So I can tell you the two cards use the same model name for different things, and I cannot tell you which one describes what you actually get, because Google does not say.

What I can add is a second source that points one way. Google's developer pricing page, a different page for a different product, lists **Gemini 4 Hydrogen as "Free of charge"** on its free tier. It also says, in its own words, that **"Gemini 4 Carbon is not available on the Free tier"** and the same for Argon.

So on the developer side, free does get Hydrogen and definitely does not get Carbon or Argon. If the consumer side works the same way, the Free card's footer is accurate and the Plus card is selling its headline feature to people who already have it.

I would like to be able to resolve this for you properly. I cannot, because resolving it requires Google to say what its own footer line means.

## Nobody tells you the version

The Google card's actual feature bullet says "our Flash-Lite model." Not Flash-Lite 3.5, which is what the developer page prices. Just the family.

Anthropic does the same thing, and in their case I can show you exactly what it costs you to not know.

Their pricing page marks the Free plan like this: **Sonnet Yes, Haiku Yes, Opus No, Fable No.** That is clearer than most. It is also unversioned, and Anthropic currently sells four Sonnets.

| Model | Input price per million | Context window |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 | $2 | 1M |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | $2 | 1M |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 | $3 | 1M |
| Claude Sonnet 4.5 | $3 | 200K |

The gap between the top and bottom of that list is a 50 percent price difference and a five-fold difference in how much you can put into one conversation. "Sonnet" does not tell a free user which of those they are talking to, and the context window is the single thing most likely to decide whether a free tier can do your job.

The same applies to Haiku, where the free user is choosing between a 1 million token model and a 200,000 token one, both called Haiku.

I am not claiming anyone is hiding this. Unversioned names are probably just how marketing pages get written. But it means the honest answer to "which model does free Claude use" is that the vendor does not publish it.

## What Anthropic does say clearly, to its credit

Two things on that page are more honest than the industry norm and should be said.

**"Opus | No" and "Fable | No"** against the free column is unambiguous. There is no footnote, no asterisk, no "limited access to." You do not get them.

The usage footnote is also better than most: **"How fast you reach a limit depends on the model you use, how long your chats are, and how large your files are."** No number, which is frustrating, but it explains the shape of the limit rather than pretending there is a fixed message count. Most vendors give you neither.

Opus starts at Pro, which is $17 a month billed annually or $20 monthly. Fable is stranger even on paid plans, running on pay as you go credits on Pro and only becoming included on Max and above, which I worked through in my [Claude review](/blog/claude-ai-review).

## What the free tier does give you, which is a lot

I have spent several sections on what free tiers will not tell you, so here is the other side, because a post that only finds fault is not reporting.

Google's free card is generous and the model question is the only thing wrong with it. Alongside the Flash-Lite bullet it lists image generation and editing, Deep Research, Gemini Live, Canvas and Skills. It gives you 15 GB of storage across Gmail, Drive and Photos. It even includes what the page calls "limited access to Nano Banana Pro" inside their creative studio.

That is a serious free product. Five years ago most of those were paid features at every vendor, and several of them did not exist.

Anthropic's free tier is narrower by design and honest about it. You get Sonnet and Haiku with limited usage, and the page tells you plainly that Opus and Fable are not included rather than burying it. Narrow and clear beats broad and vague, and if I had to choose which of these two free tiers I trust more to describe itself, it is that one.

So the complaint in this post is specific. It is not that free tiers are stingy, because they are not. It is that the one fact that decides whether a free tier can do your particular job, which model is behind it, is the fact most likely to be missing or stated twice in two different ways.

## Two vendors publish nothing

This part is the finding, not a gap in my research.

**Microsoft Copilot.** The copilot.com page is a sign-in screen. It names no model anywhere. Its one sentence about the free tier reads: "A free tier is available with a Microsoft account. Copilot AI helps you research, create, and get answers, with some capabilities subject to usage limits and more available through paid plans." The plans and pricing page on microsoft.com returned **404** on the day I checked.

**Perplexity.** Three different help URLs returned either 404 or the help centre index rather than an article. I could not reach a page stating which model the free tier uses.

**OpenAI** belongs in the same column for a different reason. Their consumer pricing pages returned **403** to an automated request on 16 November, the same as on 10 November. A 403 is a server declining to talk to a robot. It is not evidence about the product and I have not treated it as any.

So of five major free tiers, two do not publish the model, one cannot be read automatically, one publishes the family but not the version, and one publishes two different answers on the same page.

## The API free tier is not the app free tier

One correction worth making to the way this subject is usually written, including by me in earlier posts.

Google's developer pricing page and Google's consumer subscriptions page are different products. The developer page marks Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite as free of charge, and marks Carbon, Argon and Omni 1.2 Flash as not available on free. The consumer page sells plans in rupees with cloud storage attached and a different vocabulary entirely.

A sentence about one tells you nothing reliable about the other. When an article says "Gemini is free," ask which Gemini, on which product.

## How to find out what you are actually on

Three things that work, in order of reliability.

**Open the model picker and read the exact string.** Including the version. If the product has a picker, that is the most direct evidence available to you and it takes five seconds.

**Read the developer documentation rather than the pricing page.** This is consistently the better source. When I compared seven vendors' pricing pages against their own docs in [the pricing page versus documentation audit](/blog/ai-pricing-page-vs-docs-audit), the docs were more precise nearly every time, because a pricing page is written to be read and documentation is written to be correct.

**Do not ask the model which model it is.** Models are frequently wrong about their own version. The picker and the docs are evidence. A chatbot's description of itself is not.

The same caution applies to the articles you read about these models. When I checked ten pieces ranking for the best AI coding model, six of the seven that loaded recommended a model that had already been replaced, and the prices beside those recommendations were still correct, which is what makes them hard to spot. That audit is in [six of seven ranking articles name a model that has been replaced](/blog/ai-model-articles-stale-audit).

## Why this is getting harder, not easier

Both vendors in this piece shipped a new model generation in the last ten weeks. Anthropic moved to a 5.5 generation, Google moved through its Gemini 4 line, and the free tiers moved underneath without an announcement that most users would see.

That is the actual mechanism. Nobody downgrades a free tier in public. The lineup rotates, the marketing page keeps saying "Sonnet" or "Flash-Lite," and what sits behind the name changes. I tracked the same effect on paid model access in [the 2026 model retirements](/blog/ai-model-retirements-2026), and on what the vendors are about to charge in [the price rises three vendors have already published](/blog/ai-api-price-increases-december-2026).

If you want the practical version of which free tools are worth using rather than which model sits behind them, [the twelve best free AI tools](/blog/best-free-ai-tools) and [what free actually means](/blog/what-free-means-ai-tools) answer that question instead.
