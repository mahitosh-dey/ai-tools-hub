## Step 3: Your First Generation

In the web interface, click the text bar at the bottom of the screen and type a prompt. Keep it simple for your first attempt:

```
mountain lake at sunrise, reflection in still water, golden light, landscape photography
```

Press Enter. Midjourney will generate four image variations in a grid. This takes 30–60 seconds on Fast Mode.

Once the four images appear, you have several options:

**Upscale buttons (U1, U2, U3, U4)** — Select any of the four images and upscale it to a larger, higher-detail version. This is what you download. Always upscale before downloading — the grid shows small previews.

**Variation buttons (V1, V2, V3, V4)** — Generate four new variations of a specific image, keeping similar composition and mood but changing details.

**Redo button** — Generate four entirely new variations from the same prompt. Different from variations — this is a fresh generation.

Click U on the image you like best. Wait 20–30 seconds. Download the full-resolution result.

## Step 4: How Prompting Actually Works

This is where most beginners either click or keep getting frustrated results. The core principle is simple:

**Midjourney produces what you describe. Not what you mean. Not what you imagine. What you describe.**

If your description is vague, the result is generic. If your description is specific, the result is specific.

### The Four-Part Prompt Structure

Good prompts consistently describe:

**1. Subject** — what's in the image?
- Weak: "a person"
- Strong: "young woman with short dark hair, looking out a rain-covered window"

**2. Style / Medium** — what kind of image is it?
- Photography, oil painting, digital illustration, 3D render, watercolour, concept art, anime, comic book, product photography, editorial fashion photography

**3. Lighting and Mood** — what does it feel like?
- Golden hour, overcast diffused light, neon glow, moody dramatic shadows, soft studio light, cinematic, dark and atmospheric, bright and airy

**4. Technical Specifics** — aspect ratio, camera type, compositional details
- Close-up portrait, wide angle, bird's eye view, shallow depth of field, 35mm film grain

### Examples: Weak vs. Strong

| Weak Prompt | Strong Prompt |
|-------------|---------------|
| "a city at night" | "aerial view of Tokyo at night, neon signs reflecting on wet streets, dramatic lighting, cinematic, --ar 16:9" |
| "a cat" | "portrait of a tabby cat sitting on a windowsill, soft morning light, shallow depth of field, candid photography style, --ar 4:5" |
| "a coffee cup" | "product photography of a black coffee cup on marble surface, minimalist composition, soft shadow, editorial style, --ar 1:1" |

The pattern: the strong prompts describe a specific scene in a specific style with specific lighting. The weak prompts give Midjourney nowhere specific to go.

### Style Keywords That Work Well

**Photography styles:** editorial photography, documentary, street photography, fashion photography, commercial product photography, portrait photography, architectural photography, wildlife photography, 35mm film, medium format

**Art styles:** oil painting, watercolour illustration, pencil sketch, digital painting, concept art, anime/manga, pixel art, Art Deco, impressionist, surrealist, minimalist

**Rendering:** photorealistic, 3D render, octane render, clay render, low poly, cinematic render, hyperrealistic

**Mood/lighting:** cinematic lighting, golden hour, blue hour, moody, dramatic, ethereal, dark and atmospheric, warm and cozy, cold and stark, soft diffused light, neon-lit

## Step 5: Essential Parameters

Parameters go at the end of your prompt after the description. They use double dashes (--).

### Aspect Ratio — `--ar`

The most commonly used parameter. Sets image dimensions.

```
coastal village, aerial view, Mediterranean architecture --ar 16:9
```

| Ratio   | Use Case |
|---------|----------|
| `--ar 1:1` | Square (default), social media posts |
| `--ar 16:9` | Blog headers, YouTube thumbnails, desktop wallpapers |
| `--ar 9:16` | Instagram Stories, TikTok, phone wallpapers |
| `--ar 4:5` | Instagram feed, portrait prints |
| `--ar 3:2` | Standard photography, prints |

**Set your aspect ratio first, before generating.** Generating a square image and cropping to 16:9 later wastes detail and resolution.

### Style — `--style raw`

Reduces Midjourney's automatic aesthetic "beautification" and produces a more neutral interpretation of your prompt. Useful when the default output is too polished or stylized.

```
weathered wooden barn in a field --style raw
```

### Chaos — `--chaos`

A value from 0–100 controlling how varied the four initial results are. Low values (10–20) produce similar, focused results. High values (60–80) produce dramatically different variations. Good for exploration.

```
abstract fluid art, vibrant colours --chaos 70
```

### No — `--no`

Exclusion parameter. Tells Midjourney what to leave out.

```
forest path in autumn, misty morning --no people --no animals
```

### Weird — `--weird`

A value from 0–3000 that pushes Midjourney toward unconventional, unexpected aesthetics. Start at 250–500 if you want something unusual without losing coherence.

```
portrait, ethereal --weird 500
```

### Seed — `--seed`

Using a specific seed number makes Midjourney more likely to reproduce a similar result. Useful when you've found a result you like and want to generate close variations by changing the prompt slightly.

```
neon cityscape --seed 12345
```

You find the seed number of any image in the web interface by clicking on the image and checking its properties.

## Step 6: Using the Editor After Generation

One of the most underused features for beginners: Midjourney's built-in editor lets you modify images after they're generated, without starting over from scratch.

**Inpainting (Vary Region)** — Select a specific area of an upscaled image and give it a new prompt. The selected area regenerates while the rest stays the same. Useful for fixing one element without redoing the whole image (wrong facial expression, mismatched item in background, etc.).

**Outpainting (Zoom Out / Pan)** — Extend the image beyond its original edges. Select "Zoom Out" after upscaling to see what Midjourney adds around the original composition. Pan left, right, up, or down to extend in a specific direction.

To access these tools: upscale an image, then look for the editing options (Vary Region, Zoom Out, Pan buttons) below the image in the web interface.

## Step 7: Getting Consistent Results

The most common beginner frustration: getting one great result, then not being able to recreate it. A few habits fix this.

**Save your prompts.** Keep a simple text file or note with the exact prompts that produced results you liked, including all parameters. The same prompt generates slightly different results each time, but starting from your best prompt is faster than rebuilding.

**Upscale everything before you decide.** The 2x2 preview grid shows small, low-resolution versions. The final upscaled image can look significantly different — sometimes better, sometimes worse. Don't skip or dismiss a result based on the preview alone.

**Use Variation before Regeneration.** When one image is close but not quite right, hit V (Variation) on that specific image rather than running the full prompt again. Variations stay closer to the selected image's composition and feel.

**Try the same prompt twice.** If the first four results miss entirely, hit the redo button and try again with the same prompt. The randomness of AI generation means the second set can be dramatically better.
